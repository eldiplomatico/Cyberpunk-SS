## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This assessment maps every publicly available claim linking specific US defense contractors to alleged programs involving the recovery and reverse-engineering of materials of anomalous or non-human origin. The evidence base consists primarily of secondhand testimony, leaked documents of disputed authenticity, congressional allegations, and circumstantial historical connections. No contractor has publicly confirmed possession of or work on non-human materials. The AARO Historical Record Report (2024) states that contractor executives explicitly denied such programs on the record. However, significant legislative and executive branch actions in 2025-2026 -- including the ODNI's PURSUE Task Force NDA waiver (July 2026), the House adoption of the UAP Disclosure Act with eminent domain provisions (July 2026), and Rep. Burlison's formal records demands to MITRE and other FFRDCs -- demonstrate that elements of Congress and the executive branch treat these claims as warranting serious investigation rather than dismissal.

**Key Finding**: The contractor angle rests on a small number of interconnected claims from a consistent group of individuals. No physical evidence has been publicly produced. However, the legislative response -- particularly the unprecedented eminent domain language targeting contractor-held materials -- implies that at least some lawmakers with access to classified briefings believe the claims have substance.

## 1. WILSON-DAVIS MEMO CLAIMS

### 1.1 Document Overview

The Wilson-Davis memo is a 15-page handwritten document purportedly recording a meeting on October 16, 2002, between Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson (recently retired as Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency) and astrophysicist Dr. Eric W. Davis. The memo describes Wilson's account of his 1997 attempt to gain access to a classified Special Access Program (SAP) connected to crash retrieval of a non-human craft.

### 1.2 The Narrative in the Memo

According to the document:

- In 1997, following a Pentagon briefing involving Dr. Steven Greer (Disclosure Project) and astronaut Edgar Mitchell, Wilson became curious about potential UAP-related SAPs.
- Wilson pursued the matter through the Pentagon's Special Access Program Oversight Office (OUSD/SAP) over approximately two months.
- Wilson located a deeply nested program run by a private aerospace contractor.
- Wilson contacted the program manager, asserting his oversight authority as Deputy Director of Intelligence for the Joint Chiefs (J-2).
- The program manager agreed to a meeting, at which Wilson was told the program involved reverse engineering of "an alien craft -- not made by man, not by human hands, not of this world."
- Progress on the reverse engineering was described as "painfully slow."
- Wilson requested formal access and was denied by the program's gatekeepers (described as a "watch committee" of corporate and senior government figures).
- Wilson was warned of career repercussions if he continued pressing.
- The SAP compartment, code name, and the identity of the overseeing contractor or government agency were described as "core secrets."

### 1.3 Implied Contractor

The memo does not explicitly name the contractor. However, researcher Richard Dolan has assessed the implied contractor as **Lockheed Martin**, based on:
- The memo's references to a large aerospace prime contractor
- The program's described relationship with USAF and DIA oversight structures
- Circumstantial connections to Lockheed's Skunk Works division and its history with advanced classified programs

| Assessment Element | Rating |
|---|---|
| Source Tier | Tier 3 (leaked document of disputed authenticity) |
| Firsthand/Secondhand | Secondhand -- notes allegedly recording Wilson's firsthand account, written by Davis |
| Corroboration Level | Partial -- chain of custody established; congressional interest confirmed |
| Entity Denial | Wilson has denied the document; Davis has implicitly confirmed authorship |

### 1.4 Provenance and Leak

- The document was uploaded to Imgur on April 19, 2019, and circulated in the UFO research community by early June 2019.
- Researcher Grant Cameron announced he had obtained it from the estate of the late astronaut Edgar Mitchell (d. 2016).
- Australian researcher James Rigney confirmed the document was found among Mitchell's personal papers. Mitchell's children were reportedly going to discard the papers, but a close associate (nicknamed "Spaceman") retained them and subsequently shared them with Rigney.
- On May 17, 2022, Rep. Mike Gallagher entered the Wilson-Davis memo into the Congressional Record, elevating it from community rumor to a document of formal congressional interest.

### 1.5 Wilson's Position

Vice Admiral Thomas Wilson has **consistently denied** the document's claims:
- He characterizes the memo as false.
- He acknowledges meeting Edgar Mitchell at the Pentagon but denies undertaking any investigation of a secret UAP program.
- He told journalist Billy Cox: "I didn't check or follow up."
- He denies meeting Eric Davis for the conversation described.

### 1.6 Davis's Position

Dr. Eric W. Davis has **implicitly confirmed** authorship:
- In a Facebook post, Davis referred to "our meeting" and "my interview notes" in language clearly referencing the memo.
- Davis has never issued a formal denial of the document.
- He has refrained from explicit public confirmation, citing his security clearances.
- His shift from refusing comment to implicit acknowledgment was documented by researcher Richard Dolan.

### 1.7 Assessment

The Wilson-Davis memo remains the single most detailed claim linking a specific contractor to a reverse-engineering program. Its evidential weight is limited by: (a) Wilson's denial, (b) the lack of independent verification of the events described, and (c) the secondhand nature of even Davis's account (Wilson describing events to Davis). However, Davis's implicit confirmation of authorship, the document's entry into the Congressional Record, and its consistency with other claims give it continuing significance in the discourse.

## 2. DAVID GRUSCH'S CONTRACTOR REFERENCES

### 2.1 Overview of Claims

David Grusch, a former USAF officer and intelligence official who served on the National Reconnaissance Office and the UAP Task Force, made public claims beginning in June 2023 (via NewsNation) and in sworn congressional testimony on July 26, 2023, that:

- The U.S. federal government, in collaboration with private aerospace companies, operates highly secretive special access programs involving the recovery and reverse engineering of "non-human" spacecraft.
- Recoveries of "partial fragments through and up to intact vehicles" have been made for decades by the government, its allies, and defense contractors.
- The U.S. has retrieved "non-human" biological matter from craft occupants.
- Aerospace and weapons manufacturers were "siphoning money off of government contracts" and funneling it into unsanctioned research programs in advanced technology.
- Some programs are funded through Independent Research and Development (IR&D) agreements with private companies, specifically to conceal them from congressional oversight.

### 2.2 Specific Contractor References

Grusch did **not** publicly name specific contractors in his congressional testimony or media interviews. His key statements on specifics:

- "I do know the names [of Special Access Programs on UFOs], but once again, I can't discuss that publicly."
- "In a closed setting, I could tell you the specific tradecraft used" to evade oversight.
- He stated he had provided specific contractor and program names to the Intelligence Community Inspector General and the DoD Inspector General.
- He indicated willingness to provide these details in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) to cleared members of Congress.

### 2.3 Inspector General Complaint

- Grusch filed a "Disclosure of Urgent Concern(s); Complaint of Reprisal" with the Intelligence Community Inspector General on May 25, 2022.
- The ICIG assessed the complaint as **"credible and urgent"** in July 2022.
- A summary was transmitted to the Director of National Intelligence and the congressional intelligence committees (HPSCI and SSCI).
- **Important caveat**: The "credible and urgent" finding pertains to the procedural claim that Congress was being denied access to information, not a validation of the underlying claims about non-human technology.

### 2.4 SCIF Briefings and Classified Activities

- On January 13, 2024, House Oversight Committee members received a classified ICIG briefing related to Grusch's complaint.
- In 2025, classified SCIF briefings with Grusch, Luis Elizondo, Christopher Mellon, and AARO officials were scheduled.
- The contents of classified briefings remain non-public.
- Multiple members of Congress have publicly stated they found the classified information compelling, though none have disclosed specific contractor names from those sessions.

### 2.5 Appointment as Congressional Advisor (2025)

- On March 27, 2025, David Grusch was appointed Special Advisor to Rep. Eric Burlison (R-MO) for a four-month term beginning April 1, 2025.
- The appointment included reinstatement of Grusch's security clearance, which had been revoked (Grusch alleged this was retaliatory).
- Grusch advises the Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets, primarily on UAP-related topics.

### 2.6 DoD IG Whistleblower Reprisal Investigation

- A heavily redacted DoD OIG report released January 7, 2026, revealed a UAP-related whistleblower reprisal investigation centered on the revocation of classified access.
- The investigation was closed in February 2025.
- The IG concluded the complaint was **not substantiated** -- finding that the disclosures themselves did not drive the access revocation decision.
- The complainant is not named but the chronology and subject matter align with Grusch's case.

## 3. LOCKHEED MARTIN

### 3.1 Ben Rich Quotes

Ben Rich served as the second director of Lockheed's Skunk Works (Advanced Development Programs) from 1975 until his retirement in 1991. He died in 1995. Multiple quotes attributed to him have become central to UAP lore.

#### 3.1.1 The "Take ET Home" Statement

**What is documented**:
- On March 23, 1993, Rich gave a speech at UCLA School of Engineering to alumni. He concluded the presentation with the line: "We now have the technology to take ET home."
- Jan Harzan (later executive director of MUFON) and Tom Keller (UCLA engineering alumnus) attended the lecture and reported the quote.
- After the speech, Harzan and Keller approached Rich. Harzan reported Rich said: "We know how to travel to the stars. We found an error in the equations and it won't take a lifetime to do it." When pressed on propulsion, Rich asked: "How does ESP work?"

**Critical context**:
- Rich first used the "take ET home" line in **1983**, shortly after the release of Steven Spielberg's film *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial*. It received laughs and he continued using it as a reliable audience-pleasing closer.
- The line is therefore demonstrably a recurring joke, not a spontaneous disclosure.
- The post-speech exchange with Harzan/Keller rests on Harzan's personal account (secondhand, reported years later).

#### 3.1.2 Other Attributed Statements

- "We already have the means to travel among the stars, but these technologies are locked up in black projects and it would take an Act of God to ever get them out to benefit humanity." -- Attributed to the 1993 UCLA speech. The exact wording varies across sources.
- Various "deathbed confession" claims exist but lack contemporaneous documentation or independent witnesses.

### 3.2 Steve Justice and TTSA

- Steve Justice retired as Director of Advanced Systems at Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works program.
- He joined To The Stars Academy of Arts & Science (TTSA), the organization founded by Tom DeLonge in 2017, bringing significant aerospace credibility.
- TTSA launched the ADAM Project (Acquisition & Data Analysis of Materials) in July 2018 to analyze materials reportedly from advanced aircraft of unknown origin.
- Justice's departure from Lockheed to join a UAP-focused organization was noted as significant but does not itself constitute evidence of Lockheed possessing anomalous materials.

### 3.3 Harry Reid's Statements

- The late Senator Harry Reid (D-NV) publicly expressed his belief that Lockheed Martin may have possessed UFO fragments or materials.
- Reid made these statements in multiple interviews before his death in 2021.
- Reid was instrumental in funding the AAWSAP/AATIP programs through the DIA.
- His statements are notable for their source (a senior senator with access to classified programs) but remain his personal belief rather than documented evidence.

### 3.4 Wilson-Davis Memo Implication

As detailed in Section 1, researchers have identified Lockheed Martin as the most likely contractor referenced in the Wilson-Davis memo, though the memo does not name the company.

### 3.5 Overall Lockheed Martin Assessment

Lockheed Martin is the most frequently named contractor in UAP materials claims. The evidence consists of: attributed quotes from a deceased executive (Rich), the career move of a senior engineer (Justice), the belief of a deceased senator (Reid), and the analytical inference from a disputed document (Wilson-Davis). Lockheed Martin has remained silent on these claims and has not issued specific denials. According to the AARO report, contractor executives -- which would include Lockheed Martin leadership if they were among those interviewed -- denied possessing extraterrestrial materials.

## 4. BATTELLE MEMORIAL INSTITUTE

### 4.1 Historical Context

Battelle Memorial Institute, headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, is a private nonprofit applied science and technology development company. It has operated or co-managed several US national laboratories and has longstanding contracts with the Department of Defense and intelligence community.

### 4.2 Roswell Debris Analysis Claims

The core claim, advanced primarily by researcher Anthony Bragalia, is that Wright-Patterson Air Force Base contracted Battelle to analyze material recovered from the 1947 Roswell incident.

#### 4.2.1 Elroy John Center

- Elroy John Center was a Battelle scientist.
- In June 1960, Center privately told associates that he had analyzed metal from a crashed UFO while at Battelle.
- This claim was first publicly reported in 1992.
- A 1949 Battelle study on titanium alloys was obtained under FOIA in August 2009. Center was identified as a co-author (listed as "et al") of the study.
- Center's section was entitled "Analytical Methods for the Titanium Base Alloy."
- The timing (post-Roswell) and subject matter (exotic titanium alloys) are presented as circumstantially significant.

#### 4.2.2 Dr. Howard C. Cross

- Dr. Howard C. Cross was a research metallurgist at Battelle who simultaneously served as:
  - Battelle's lead on classified titanium alloy studies contracted by Wright-Patterson AFB
  - Battelle's "point person" for UFO studies conducted for the USAF's Project Blue Book
- Cross authored the controversial "Pentacle Memo" (1953), a letter to Wright-Patterson concerning UFO research methodology.
- Bragalia and others argue that Cross's dual role -- directing secret titanium research while also serving as Battelle's UFO liaison -- is not coincidental.

### 4.3 The Nitinol Connection

The central claim linking Battelle to Roswell through Nitinol:

1. Roswell witnesses described debris with "memory metal" properties -- material that returned to its original shape after being deformed.
2. In the late 1940s, Wright-Patterson contracted Battelle to study nickel-titanium (NiTi) alloys.
3. Four Wright-Patterson-sponsored technical studies on "memory metal" in subsequent decades cited a 1949 Battelle report on NiTi alloys.
4. Cross allegedly "seeded" titanium alloy research to the US Naval Ordnance Laboratory, where Nitinol (Nickel Titanium Naval Ordnance Laboratory) was officially "discovered" in 1959-1962 by William Buehler.
5. The theory: Battelle's analysis of Roswell debris exhibiting memory-metal properties directly inspired the development of Nitinol.

**Critical assessment**: This chain of reasoning is circumstantial. The development of Nitinol has a documented conventional history. The connection relies on: (a) accepting that Roswell debris exhibited memory-metal properties (witness testimony only), (b) that Battelle analyzed this debris (Center's private claim, partially supported by FOIA documents showing he worked on relevant alloys at the right time), and (c) that the research was deliberately channeled to the Navy (Cross's dual role is documented, but the "seeding" claim is inferential).

| Assessment Element | Rating |
|---|---|
| Source Tier | Tier 3 (mix of FOIA-obtained documents, private statements by now-deceased scientists, and researcher inference) |
| Firsthand/Secondhand | Mixed -- Center's claim is firsthand (his own experience) but reported secondhand; the documentary evidence (FOIA-obtained studies) is primary source material but requires interpretive leaps |
| Corroboration Level | Partial -- FOIA documents confirm Battelle's work on NiTi alloys for Wright-Patterson in the relevant timeframe; the UFO connection is inferential |
| Entity Denial | Battelle has not publicly addressed these claims |

## 6. AARO CONTRACTOR INTERVIEWS

### 6.1 Methodology

The AARO Historical Record Report Volume I (released March 8, 2024) describes the following methodology:
- Reviewed all official USG investigatory efforts since 1945
- Researched classified and unclassified archives
- Conducted approximately 30 interviews
- Partnered with IC and DoD officials responsible for controlled and special access program oversight

### 6.2 Contractor Executive Interviews

The report states:

> "The executives, scientists, and chief technology officers of the companies named by interviewees met with the Director of AARO and denied on the record that they have ever recovered, possessed, or engaged in reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial technology."

**Critical observations**:
- The report does not name the specific companies whose executives were interviewed.
- The report does not describe the methodology of these interviews (duration, scope of questions, whether conducted under oath).
- The report does not address whether the interviewees would have had sufficient clearance or need-to-know to be aware of such programs even if they existed within their organizations.
- The report does not address the structural possibility that compartmented programs could exist below the awareness level of C-suite executives.

### 6.3 CIA Official Interview

In one specific case:
- Interviewees claimed a former CIA official was involved with a reverse-engineering program from 2009-2010.
- AARO interviewed this former CIA official, who denied the claims.
- The same former CIA officer signed a memo explicitly rejecting the claim that he managed the movement of and experimentation on "off-world technology."

### 6.4 KONA BLUE

- AARO's investigation identified KONA BLUE as a proposed DHS program that would have restarted UAP investigations, paranormal research, and reverse-engineering of recovered spacecraft.
- KONA BLUE traced its origins to the DIA-managed AAWSAP/AATIP program.
- The program was never formally established, received no funding, and no materials were transferred.
- DHS rejected the proposal citing lack of merit.

### 6.5 Overall AARO Assessment

The AARO report concluded: "No empirical evidence for claims that the USG and private companies have been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology."

**Criticisms of the AARO investigation**:
- Critics argue AARO lacked the authority and clearances to access deeply compartmented programs.
- The approximately 30 interviews were characterized as insufficient by UAP researchers.
- The report's reliance on executive-level denials was criticized as inadequate given the alleged compartmentalization of any such programs.
- Multiple members of Congress publicly expressed dissatisfaction with the report's thoroughness.
- AARO received 262 submissions through its secure reporting mechanism from individuals claiming firsthand knowledge, with only seven identified for follow-up interviews.

## 7. SAP/USAP STRUCTURE AND OVERSIGHT

### 7.1 Types of Special Access Programs

The US government operates a tiered system of classified program protection:

1. **Acknowledged SAPs**: Their existence is publicly known, though details are classified. Subject to standard congressional reporting requirements.

2. **Unacknowledged SAPs (USAPs)**: Their existence is classified. By federal statute, all active acknowledged and non-waived unacknowledged SAPs must submit reports to the House and Senate Authorization, Appropriations, and Intelligence Committees annually.

3. **Waived Unacknowledged SAPs (Waived USAPs or WUSAPs)**: These are formally exempted from the majority of oversight and reporting requirements by the Secretary of Defense or the President. Only the "Gang of Eight" -- the chairpersons and ranking members of the Senate and House Appropriations and Armed Services Committees -- are required to be informed.

### 7.2 Governance Structure

- The SAP Oversight Committee (SAPOC) provides multi-level oversight.
- The Senior Review Group (SRG) provides senior-level review.
- The SAP Senior Working Group (SSWG) handles operational coordination.
- The Pentagon's OUSD/SAP office administers program oversight.

### 7.3 Could a Materials Program Exist Without Congressional Notification?

**Legal requirements**:
- Standard USAPs: Must be reported to multiple congressional committees annually.
- Waived USAPs: Notification limited to Gang of Eight, but notification is still legally required.
- The National Security Act requires notification to congressional intelligence committees of covert activities.

**Theoretical vulnerabilities**:
- Grusch's allegation of IR&D (Independent Research and Development) funding suggests a mechanism by which contractor-internal programs could avoid government reporting requirements entirely. IR&D is company-funded research that is reimbursed through overhead on government contracts but is not subject to the same oversight as government-directed programs.
- A program structured as contractor proprietary work rather than a government SAP would fall outside the SAP oversight framework entirely.
- Waived USAPs, while legally requiring Gang of Eight notification, could theoretically persist with incomplete or misleading notifications.
- The Wilson-Davis memo describes exactly this scenario: a program structured to deny access even to the DIA director.

### 7.4 Structural Assessment

The SAP framework includes mechanisms that could, in theory, be exploited to conceal a program from normal oversight. The most plausible concealment vectors are:
1. Contractor-internal programs funded through IR&D or other private mechanisms
2. Waived USAPs with extremely restricted notification
3. Legacy programs predating modern oversight requirements that were never brought into compliance
4. Programs mischaracterized to oversight bodies (e.g., described as conventional materials science rather than reverse-engineering)

## 8. SCHUMER-ROUNDS UAP DISCLOSURE ACT

### 8.1 Original Proposal (2023)

Senators Chuck Schumer (D-NY) and Mike Rounds (R-SD) introduced the UAP Disclosure Act as Senate Amendment 797 to the FY2024 NDAA in July 2023. Key provisions:

- **Eminent domain**: The federal government would exercise eminent domain over "any and all recovered technologies of unknown origin and biological evidence of non-human intelligence that may be controlled by private persons or entities in the interests of the public good."
- **UAP Records Collection**: Establishment of an official government collection of UAP records.
- **Review Board**: Creation of an independent review board with power over UAP-related materials held by private contractors.
- **Declassification timeline**: Government records on UAP to be released within 25 years of creation unless national security requires continued classification.
- **Positive presumption of disclosure**: Records presumed releasable unless specifically exempted.

### 8.2 Legislative Significance

The eminent domain provision was without precedent in intelligence legislation. Its inclusion operationally assumes the existence of recovered technology under corporate custody -- a remarkable legislative assumption.

### 8.3 FY2024 Outcome

- The amendment passed the Senate 84-0.
- During House-Senate conference negotiations, the eminent domain provisions, Review Board powers, and positive presumption mechanism were **stripped**.
- The final FY2024 NDAA retained a scaled-back version establishing a UAP Records Collection at NARA (Record Group 615) but without enforcement mechanisms against private holders.
- The stripping was attributed to House resistance, with some reporting linking opposition to defense industry lobbying.

### 8.4 FY2026 Attempt

- Schumer, Rounds, and Gillibrand filed the "UAP Disclosure Act of 2025" as amendment SA 3111 to the FY2026 NDAA.
- Nearly identical to the 2023 version, including eminent domain provisions.
- The amendment was **not included** in the final House-Senate conference agreement for FY2026.

### 8.5 FY2027 Breakthrough (July 2026)

- On July 22, 2026, the House adopted Rep. Eric Burlison's version of the UAP Disclosure Act as an amendment to the FY2027 NDAA.
- This version **includes the eminent domain provision** -- the first time it has survived a House floor vote.
- As of September 2026, the FY2027 NDAA is in the conference process; whether the provision survives conference remains to be determined.

## 9. RECENT DEVELOPMENTS (2024-2026)

### 9.1 PURSUE Task Force (2026)

- President Trump directed the creation of the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE) Task Force.
- Launched May 1, 2026, under the Department of War.
- Coordinates identification, review, and release of UAP-related records across the Intelligence Community.

### 9.2 ODNI NDA Waiver (July 31, 2026)

- The ODNI issued preliminary guidance directing that current and former government employees and contractors with information on UAPs shall have prior NDAs, oaths, and commitments **waived** when speaking with AARO or PURSUE representatives.
- This represents total legal immunity for UAP whistleblowers speaking through authorized channels.
- Contractors can no longer face prosecution, clearance revocation, or civil penalties for disclosing classified UAP data to AARO/PURSUE.
- All IC agencies must designate a PURSUE representative within 30 days.
- **This is the most significant development for the contractor angle**: it removes the primary enforcement mechanism (NDAs and secrecy oaths) that would have prevented contractor employees from disclosing involvement in UAP programs.

### 9.3 PURSUE Document Releases

- Beginning May 8, 2026, the government released declassified UAP files through the PURSUE database at war.gov/ufo.
- Five tranches released as of September 2026 (May 8, May 22, June 12, and two additional).
- Content includes reports, photographs, videos, witness accounts, military records, and astronaut transcripts from 1944-45 to recent years.
- Reaction from the UAP research community has been mixed; critics noted "data alone is not disclosure" and called for more substantive releases regarding alleged programs.

### 9.4 Rep. Burlison's MITRE Demand (May 2026)

- 10-page formal demand to MITRE Corporation for all UAP-related records dating to 1930.
- Requests cover SAPs, Controlled Access Programs, classification guides, NDA instruments, contractor-held federal records, sensor data, materials analysis, and budgetary mechanisms.
- MITRE did not deny holding relevant material; stated it was reviewing archives.
- Required MITRE to designate a senior official, issue a preservation hold, produce a records location index, and coordinate classified briefings.
- Burlison also named 12+ other contractors in his inquiry scope.

### 9.5 Immaculate Constellation (2024)

- In late 2024, journalist Michael Shellenberger revealed allegations of a Pentagon UAP program called "Immaculate Constellation."
- Alleged to be an unacknowledged SAP created in 2017 that houses classified UAP evidence (videos, images, documentation).
- The DoD denied any record of a SAP called "Immaculate Constellation."
- A whistleblower report alleged DOD and private defense contractors are spending billions to reverse engineer UAP technology.

## 11. KEY ANALYTICAL JUDGMENTS

### 11.1 The Contractor Nexus

The claim that private defense contractors possess and are reverse-engineering non-human materials is supported by:
- **For**: Grusch's ICIG-validated complaint; the Wilson-Davis memo (if authentic); multiple members of Congress behaving as though classified evidence exists; the unprecedented eminent domain legislation specifically targeting contractor-held materials; the ODNI NDA waiver for contractor employees; Battelle's documented NiTi research in the relevant timeframe.
- **Against**: AARO's investigation finding no evidence; contractor executive denials; no publicly produced physical evidence; the "credible and urgent" ICIG finding relates to process, not substance; the Wilson memo is denied by one of its two alleged participants; the small number of independent sources (many claims trace to the same network of individuals).

### 11.2 The Circular Evidence Problem

A significant analytical challenge is that many of the claims are mutually reinforcing but trace to a small community of interconnected individuals. The AARO report explicitly noted that modern claims could be "traced to a consistent group of individuals tied to a cancelled DIA effort [AAWSAP/AATIP] and the failed DHS proposal KONA BLUE." This does not prove the claims are false, but it limits the independence of corroboration.

### 11.3 The Legislative Signal

The strongest indicator that the contractor claims may have substance is the behavior of legislators with access to classified information:
- The 84-0 Senate vote for the original UAPDA
- The repeated introduction of eminent domain provisions
- Rep. Burlison's increasingly aggressive records demands
- The appointment of Grusch as a congressional advisor with restored clearance
- The ODNI NDA waiver specifically covering contractor employees

These actions are not proof of the underlying claims, but they represent costly signals that are difficult to explain as purely performative.

### 11.4 The Disclosure Trajectory

As of September 2026, the trajectory is toward increased transparency:
- The PURSUE NDA waiver removes the primary barrier to contractor employee disclosure
- The House adoption of the UAPDA with eminent domain language (July 2026) is the furthest this provision has advanced
- MITRE's response to Burlison's demand is pending
- Grusch continues to advise Congress with restored clearances
- Multiple additional whistleblowers have reportedly come forward through AARO's secure mechanism

Whether this trajectory produces definitive evidence regarding contractor-held materials remains to be seen.
