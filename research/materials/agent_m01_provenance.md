## SECTION 1: "Art's Parts" / Bismuth-Magnesium-Zinc Layered Material

### 1.1 What It Allegedly Is

A multilayered metallic specimen composed of alternating micron-scale layers of bismuth and magnesium-zinc alloy. The bismuth layers are described as thinner than a human hair; the magnesium-zinc layers approximately ten times thicker. The material exhibits a columnar grain structure perpendicular to the banding. It has been claimed to originate from a crashed extraterrestrial vehicle, specifically linked to the 1947 Roswell incident by the anonymous source.

### 1.2 Alleged Recovery and Original Source

**Date of Receipt:** April 1996
**Received by:** Art Bell, host of Coast to Coast AM radio program
**Method of delivery:** Two shipments of metal pieces sent by postal mail, accompanied by five typed letters
**Postmark origin:** South Carolina
**Anonymous sender's claim:** The sender identified himself only as a currently serving military member with a security clearance. He claimed the materials came from a box belonging to his grandfather, who he alleged was a member of a retrieval team sent to the Roswell crash site in 1947. The sender provided what he said was based on his grandfather's diary.
**Sender identity:** Never publicly revealed. The correspondence remained anonymous throughout.
**Source quality for origin claim:** Tier 4 (anonymous letter, unverifiable provenance)

### 1.4 Analyses Performed

#### Analysis 1: Nicholas A. Reiter (commissioned by Linda Moulton Howe)
- **Date:** 1996 (initial), 2001 (update)
- **Analyst:** Nicholas A. Reiter, technologist (now at Marquette University Chemistry Department)
- **Method:** Cut and polished cross-section; energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS); SEM imaging; water immersion test
- **Results (1996):** Confirmed composition of magnesium, bismuth, and zinc. Described layered structure with bismuth (white lines in SEM) alternating with magnesium-zinc alloy. Noted unusual properties including colored spheres visible under magnification and hexagonal structure. A 90mg sample in distilled water showed magnesium ions at 60x normal concentration with hydrogen bubbles after 40 days. Concluded the material was "earthly and, while unusual, was not impossible."
- **Results (2001 update):** Identified the Betterton-Kroll Process -- an industrial lead-refining technique -- as a plausible terrestrial explanation. This process uses molten magnesium floated over liquid lead to extract bismuth impurities, producing bismuth-magnesium layered byproducts.
- **Replication attempt:** Howe asked Reiter to reproduce the structure. He succeeded, though Howe reportedly rejected the result because it did not look exactly like the original pieces and was not as thick.
- **Independence from Puthoff-Davis-Green-Bigelow-Vallee network:** YES -- Reiter was an independent technologist with no known affiliation to this network.
- **Source quality:** Tier 3 (named analyst, documented methodology, but not peer-reviewed publication)

#### Analysis 2: TTSA / Hal Puthoff (pre-CRADA)
- **Date:** ~2017-2019
- **Analyst:** Hal Puthoff, TTSA physicist; EarthTech International / Institute for Advanced Studies at Austin
- **Method:** Not publicly detailed
- **Results:** TTSA publicly claimed the materials were "metamaterials" with potential defense applications, specifically as an electromagnetic waveguide. Technical details were not published.
- **Independence from network:** NO -- Puthoff is a central node in the Puthoff-Davis-Green-Bigelow-Vallee network. He served as Chairman of the Science Advisory Board of Bigelow Aerospace, co-founded TTSA, and was principal investigator at SRI for CIA remote viewing programs alongside Eric Davis at EarthTech International.
- **Source quality:** Tier 2 (SEC filings reference claims; Tier 3 for technical assertions made in press releases without supporting data)

#### Analysis 3: US Army DEVCOM GVSC (CRADA)
- **Date:** 2019-2024 (five-year agreement, CRADA #19-15, "Novel & Emerging Technology Exploitation")
- **Analyst:** US Army Combat Capabilities Development Command, Ground Vehicle Systems Center
- **Method:** Materials characterization (specific methods in CRADA final report, partially released via FOIA through The Black Vault)
- **Results:** Assessed that the physical and elemental properties of Artifact A1 are "incompatible in its current state to serve as an EM waveguide." Negative finding on the core TTSA claim.
- **Key limitation:** Only ONE artifact (A1) was provided by TTSA for the entire five-year duration, despite TTSA's public claims of possessing multiple samples.
- **Independence from network:** YES -- US Army laboratory operating under formal government agreement
- **Source quality:** Tier 1 (government CRADA documentation, FOIA-released)

#### Analysis 4: Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) / AARO
- **Date:** Contracted 2022; results published April 2024 with AARO supplement
- **Analyst:** Oak Ridge National Laboratory (a US Department of Energy national laboratory)
- **Method:** Elemental composition analysis; isotopic ratio measurements (magnesium and lead isotopes); high-resolution elemental mapping; structural characterization via microscopy and spectrometry; terahertz waveguide theoretical assessment
- **Results:**
  - Material is a magnesium (Mg) alloy, primarily Mg and Zn, with Bi colocated with nearly equal parts lead (Pb)
  - High-resolution mapping showed repeating layers of Bi and Pb banding (NOTE: bismuth was NOT a pure layer as initially claimed -- lead was intermixed)
  - Zinc concentration varied 1-4 wt%
  - Columnar Mg grains perpendicular to the Bi/Pb banding
  - **Isotopic ratios of magnesium and lead fall within expected values for terrestrial materials** -- not extraterrestrial
  - Does NOT meet theoretical requirements to function as a terahertz waveguide (the intermixed lead precludes this)
  - Characteristics consistent with mid-20th century magnesium alloy R&D, which often used Zn, Pb, and Bi additives for corrosion resistance
  - Banding and structural features align with manufacturing techniques of that era (e.g., vapor deposition)
- **AARO assessment:** "Terrestrial in origin." "Likely a test object, a manufacturing product or byproduct, or a material component of aerospace performance studies to evaluate the properties of magnesium alloys." AARO further noted that a "separate private sector organization's recreation of this metallic sample was almost certainly conflated with claims that the aerospace industry was attempting to reverse-engineer off-world technology."
- **Independence from network:** YES -- ORNL is a US national laboratory; AARO is a DoD office. However, note that Sean Kirkpatrick, AARO's former head, left AARO in December 2023 to join ORNL as Chief Technology Officer for Defense and Intelligence Programs -- the analysis was contracted while he was at AARO and completed at ORNL after his move. Some commentators have flagged this as a potential conflict of interest.
- **Source quality:** Tier 1 (published US national laboratory analysis + official AARO supplement, both publicly available on aaro.mil)

### 1.5 Summary Assessment

The Bi-Mg-Zn layered material -- the single most analyzed alleged UAP artifact in the public domain -- has been assessed as terrestrial in origin by every independent laboratory that has examined it. Its provenance rests entirely on an anonymous 1996 letter claiming Roswell crash retrieval origin. The Betterton-Kroll industrial process provides a mundane explanation for its layered structure. The material does not function as an EM waveguide as claimed by TTSA. Its 28-year journey from anonymous letter to national laboratory analysis represents the most complete chain-of-custody documentation for any alleged UAP material.

### 4.1 Core Claims

**Who:** David Grusch, former GS-15 intelligence officer who served as the National Reconnaissance Office's representative to the UAP Task Force and as AARO's co-lead for analysis from 2019-2023.

**Initial disclosure (June 2023):** In interviews with journalists Leslie Kean and Ralph Blumenthal (The Debrief), Grusch stated he was "informed, in the course of my official duties, of a multi-decade UAP crash retrieval and reverse-engineering program" to which he was denied access.

**Congressional testimony (July 26, 2023, House Oversight Committee):**

- Stated officials reported recovering "non-human biologics" from crash sites
- Alleged the program was "deliberately concealed from Congressional oversight"
- Declined to name specific contractors or locations in open session, stating he could only elaborate in a SCIF

### 4.2 Basis of Knowledge -- Critical Distinction

**At the time of his July 2023 testimony, Grusch's claims were ALL secondhand:**
- He had NOT personally seen recovered craft or bodies
- His information was based on interviews with "over 40 witnesses" he described as having direct knowledge
- When asked about biologics: "That was the assessment of people with direct knowledge on the program I talked to, that are currently still on the program"
- He deferred many questions to classified settings

**August 2026 update (Dr. Phil interview, August 13, 2026):**
- Grusch made his clearest claim of direct exposure, stating he "personally viewed classified photos and video of recovered craft and cadavers"
- Claimed people associated with a retrieval program provided him with photographs and video
- Stressed he did NOT personally encounter the bodies or vehicles -- his direct exposure was limited to audiovisual evidence and documentation
- When asked if he could lead officials to a facility, he said he believed he could, referring to a location "out west"
- Leslie Kean separately reported Grusch viewed classified photos of multiple craft types: crescent-shaped, flattened "sunfish," egg-shaped, and boomerang designs

**No physical materials were produced or identified by Grusch in any public setting.** His claims shifted from purely secondhand (2023) to claiming direct viewing of classified imagery (2026), but he has never claimed to have physically handled or possessed any material.

### 4.3 Named Contractors

Grusch himself has not publicly named specific contractors holding materials. However, in adjacent testimony and reporting:
- Luis Elizondo has named Lockheed Martin, McDonnell Douglas, Northrop Grumman, Boeing, Raytheon, and BAE Systems as involved
- All named contractors either declined to comment or flatly denied the allegations
- Lockheed Martin: "Questions about UAPs are best addressed by the U.S. government"

**Source quality for Grusch's claims:** Tier 1 for the fact of his testimony (congressional record); Tier 3 for substance (named whistleblower with relevant credentials, but claims remain unverified by independent evidence and shifted from secondhand to direct-viewing-of-imagery over time)

## SECTION 7: Additional Material Claims in the Ecosystem

### 7.1 Ubatuba, Brazil Magnesium Fragments (1957)

- **Origin claim:** September 14, 1957; a "flying disk" allegedly exploded over the beach at Ubatuba, Sao Paulo Province, Brazil. Metal reportedly "rained down" from the explosion.
- **First report:** Ibrahim Sued, columnist for O Globo (Rio de Janeiro), received a letter and three small pieces of white metal from an anonymous witness.
- **Early analysis:** Dr. Barbosa and Elson Teixeira (Mineral Production Laboratory, Brazil) found 99.99% pure magnesium -- at the time, unusually high purity.
- **Modern analysis (Nolan, Vallee, et al.):** Isotopic analysis found magnesium isotope ratios fell within terrestrial limits, while trace element isotope ratios were inconclusive. One subsample showed magnesium isotope ratios ~30% off expected terrestrial values, while another subsample from the same event had perfectly normal ratios -- a discrepancy that remains unexplained.
- **Independence from network:** The modern analysis was conducted by Garry Nolan (Stanford) and Jacques Vallee -- Vallee is part of the network; Nolan's Stanford laboratory is independent but he collaborates with Vallee.
- **Source quality:** Tier 1 for modern peer-reviewed analysis (Progress in Aerospace Sciences, 2022); Tier 4 for original provenance (anonymous letter)

### 7.2 Council Bluffs, Iowa Material (1977)

- **Origin claim:** 1977 incident where witnesses reported a luminous object; investigators found a puddle of rapidly cooled iron.
- **Original analysis:** Ames Laboratory; X-ray fluorescence, electron beam microprobe, emission spectroscopy. Material consists of solid metal, slag, and white ash inclusions.
- **Modern analysis (Nolan, Vallee, Jiang, Lemke, 2022):** Applied secondary ion mass spectroscopy (SIMS) and multiplexed ion beam imaging (MIBI) to a surviving sample labeled CB_JV-1. Isotopic ratios for titanium, iron, and chromium were consistent with terrestrial-normal values.
- **Published in:** "Improved instrumental techniques, including isotopic analysis, applicable to the characterization of unusual materials with potential relevance to aerospace forensics," Progress in Aerospace Sciences, Vol. 128, January 2022.
- **Conclusion:** The paper did NOT claim non-human origin. It presented the case as a demonstration of modern analytical methodology for aerospace forensics. The sample's provenance and function "remain unknown."
- **Source quality:** Tier 1 (peer-reviewed journal publication)

### 7.3 Wilson-Davis Memo (Material Claims by Reference)

- **Document:** 15-page set of handwritten notes attributed to Dr. Eric Davis, dated October 16, 2002
- **Content claim:** Records retired Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson describing being denied access in 1997 to a compartmented crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering program at an unnamed defense contractor
- **Key detail about materials:** Wilson allegedly described meeting representatives at a defense contractor who confirmed they were studying "recovered alien technology from an intact craft of unknown origin." A "bigot list" of ~500 names (almost all from private contractors) controlled access.
- **Authenticity:** Contested. Wilson has called the document "fiction." Other named figures have either affirmed the account or declined comment. The Pentagon stated unfamiliarity with the memo.
- **Congressional Record:** Submitted as exhibit HHRG-117-IG05-20220517-SD001 at the May 17, 2022 House Intelligence Committee hearing by Rep. Mike Gallagher
- **Source quality:** Tier 3 (the document physically exists and is in the Congressional Record, but its authenticity is disputed by the principal named subject)

### 7.4 Vallee Material Archive (Archives of the Impossible, Rice University)

- **What:** Jacques Vallee donated his research collection to Rice University's Archives of the Impossible (AOTI) in 2018, including files on approximately 500 anomalous events he investigated.
- **Physical materials:** The archive reportedly includes physical samples Vallee collected over decades, though the full inventory is not publicly catalogued.
- **Status:** The Jacques F. Vallee UFO and paranormal phenomena papers are catalogued but listed as "not yet open" at Rice University Archives.
- **Source quality:** Tier 1 for the existence of the archive; Tier 3 for any claims about specific materials within it (not independently examined or inventoried publicly)

## SECTION 10: Key Findings and Analytical Judgments

### Finding 1: No independently verified anomalous material exists in the public record
Every physical sample that has undergone independent laboratory analysis (ORNL, Ames Laboratory, Reiter) has been assessed as terrestrial in origin. The only anomalous result in the peer-reviewed literature (one Ubatuba subsample with deviant Mg isotope ratios) is inconclusive, as another subsample from the same event showed normal ratios, and the original provenance is anonymous.

### Finding 2: A single material dominates the record
The Bi-Mg-Zn layered material ("Art's Parts") is functionally the ONLY alleged UAP material to have received sustained scientific attention spanning nearly three decades (1996-2024). Its provenance rests on an anonymous 1996 letter. It has been found terrestrial by every independent lab.

### Finding 3: Chain-of-custody failures are systemic
No claimed UAP material in the public record has an unbroken, documented chain of custody from alleged recovery to analysis. All provenance claims originate from anonymous sources, secondhand testimony, or the interconnected Puthoff-Davis-Green-Bigelow-Vallee network.

### Finding 4: The "materials" claims primarily reference materials no one can produce
The most significant claims (Grusch's crash retrieval testimony, Bigelow's Lockheed Martin negotiations, the KONA BLUE proposal) all reference materials allegedly held by defense contractors or classified programs that are inaccessible for independent analysis. The testable claims have been tested and found mundane; the extraordinary claims remain untestable.

### Finding 5: Network concentration is a significant analytical concern
The same small group of approximately 6-8 individuals appears repeatedly as custodians, analysts, advocates, and advisors across virtually every claimed UAP material in the system. This does not inherently invalidate their claims, but it means the apparent breadth of independent corroboration is significantly narrower than it appears.

### Finding 6: AARO's institutional position is clear but contested
AARO has examined available materials and found them terrestrial. Critics argue AARO was not given access to the most significant materials (those allegedly held in compartmented contractor programs). AARO's response is that no one has provided empirical evidence that such materials exist. This is a non-falsifiable impasse without either (a) production of the alleged materials or (b) formal congressional/IG access to the allegedly compartmented programs.

### Finding 7: The evidentiary gap between claims and evidence is growing, not shrinking
Between 2023 and 2026, claims escalated (Grusch progressed from secondhand testimony to claiming he viewed classified imagery; Bigelow briefed the President), but no new physical evidence has entered the public record for independent analysis. The only new analytical results (AARO/ORNL) confirmed terrestrial origin of available samples.
