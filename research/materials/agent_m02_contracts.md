## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report maps the financial, institutional, and deliverable trail of the U.S. government's documented anomalous aerospace research programs from 2008 through the present day. The central finding is that the commonly cited "$22 million Pentagon UFO program" was actually a single DIA contract (HHM402-08-C-0072) awarded to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), which produced 38 technical papers and conducted field investigations -- including at Skinwalker Ranch -- before DIA terminated the contract in 2012 citing insufficient merit. What followed was an unfunded informal effort (AATIP) inside OUSD(I), then a succession of formal offices (UAPTF, AARO) with progressively broader mandates but no confirmed custody of anomalous materials.

### 1.2 Total Contract Value

The commonly cited figure of **$22 million** is confirmed as the total appropriated across two congressional earmarks:

- **FY2008 Supplemental:** $10 million -- earmarked by Senators Harry Reid (D-NV) and Daniel Inouye (D-HI) in the July 2008 defense supplemental appropriation.
- **FY2010 Defense Budget:** $12 million -- earmarked for continuation of the DIA program.

These were **congressional direction** (earmarks), not standard DIA budget line items. No additional appropriated funding for AAWSAP has been identified in the public record.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- DIA FOIA releases; Congressional appropriations language. **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *Las Vegas Now* (KLAS/I-Team, George Knapp, 2017-2018); *New York Times* (Helene Cooper, Ralph Blumenthal, Leslie Kean, Dec. 16, 2017).

### 1.3 Solicitation and Award Circumstances

The solicitation was issued as a small-business set-aside using a Statement of Objectives (SOO) format. BAASS was the **sole bidder**. The solicitation covered 12 technical approach areas including lift, propulsion, control, power generation, materials, and signature reduction. Approaches 1-4, 6, and 9 were accepted for the initial $10 million first-year award.

The single-bidder outcome has drawn scrutiny. Senator Reid's prior relationship with Robert Bigelow -- a major campaign donor -- and the narrow solicitation scope (effectively requiring an organization already conducting anomalous aerospace research) are cited by critics as indicators of a tailored solicitation.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- Original solicitation document (Black Vault FOIA); **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *Reason* magazine (Nov. 2022); *The War Zone* / *The Drive*.

### 1.4 Deliverables: Required vs. Delivered

**Required (per SOO):**
- Approximately 2 technical reports per each of 12 technical areas (~24 minimum by July 31, 2009)
- Monthly status reports
- Project management plans

**Delivered (per BAASS 10-Month Report, July 2009):**
- 12 project management plans
- 26 detailed research reports (exceeding the ~24 minimum)
- Monthly status reports
- A 494-page "Ten Month Report" covering strategic plans, project summaries, data tables, graphs, biological effects data, detection methods, witness interviews, photographs, and case synopses
- Ongoing field investigation operations (including at Skinwalker Ranch)

**Delivered (lifetime of contract):**
- **38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs)** -- the program's signature deliverable (see Section 1.5 below)
- Field investigation reports on UAP encounters reported by military personnel
- Skinwalker Ranch investigation reports (controversial -- see Section 1.6)

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- DIA FOIA releases, contract status briefing. **BOOK/MEMOIR** -- *Skinwalkers at the Pentagon* (Lacatski, Kelleher, Knapp, 2021).

On **March 25, 2022**, DIA released 37 of the 38 DIRDs via FOIA. One document (DIRD-38, believed to relate to a classified subject) was withheld.

#### Payment Structure for DIRDs

The DIRDs were commissioned through BAASS as the prime contractor. BAASS paid subcontractors (individual scientists and their institutions) to produce the papers. The per-paper payment amounts have not been officially confirmed in FOIA releases. Some open-source reporting suggests payments in the range of **$25,000 per paper**, but this figure has not been verified against contract records.

The total subcontractor expenditure on DIRDs represented a fraction of the $22 million contract value; the majority of funds went to BAASS operational costs, field investigations, and the Skinwalker Ranch program.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- DIA FOIA release (37 DIRDs, March 2022); DIA letter to Congress (Jan. 2018). **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- KLAS/George Knapp title list release (July 2018); *The War Zone* analysis. **ACADEMIC** -- FAS archived document.

### 1.6 Materials-Analysis Component

The AAWSAP contract scope **did include materials-related work**, though the extent is debated:

**Documented:**
- DIRD topics included "Metamaterials for Aerospace Applications" and "Materials for Advanced Aerospace Platforms"
- The BAASS 10-Month Report references research on physical effects and signatures associated with UAP
- Per *The New York Times* (Dec. 2017): "Under Mr. Bigelow's direction, the company modified buildings in Las Vegas for the storage of metal alloys and other materials that [AAWSAP] participants said had been recovered from unidentified aerial phenomena"

**Unverified:**
- Claims that BAASS received and stored physical materials alleged to be of anomalous origin
- Claims that materials analysis extended beyond the DIRD literature reviews to hands-on laboratory analysis of recovered samples

**AARO's Assessment (2024):**
AARO's Historical Record Report (Vol. 1, March 2024) states that the materials associated with the program were analyzed and found to be terrestrial in origin.

**Source Tier: CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *New York Times* (Dec. 2017). **GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- AARO Historical Record Report Vol. 1 (March 2024). **BOOK/MEMOIR** -- *Skinwalkers at the Pentagon* (2021).

### 1.7 Contract Termination

The AAWSAP contract **was not renewed** after available option years expired circa 2012. DIA's stated reasons:

1. **Insufficient merit:** DIA assessed that the deliverables did not justify continued funding
2. **Mission drift:** The contractor conducted unauthorized UAP and paranormal research at Skinwalker Ranch, deviating from the official focus on advanced aerospace threat assessment
3. **Failed SAP request:** In June 2009, Sen. Reid wrote to DoD requesting AAWSAP be elevated to a Restricted Special Access Program. In November 2009, DIA Director LTG Ronald Burgess and Deputy SecDef William Lynn denied the request, stating the program's deliverables were unclassified and did not justify SAP protections. A DIA memo dated November 13, 2009, noted that "they could not justify a SAP for the program based on the unclassified nature of their deliverables and projected future products."

The denial of SAP status effectively signaled DIA's declining institutional support for the program.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- DIA FOIA releases (SAP denial memo); DIA FOIA Electronic Reading Room (dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237660/). **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *Reason* (2022).

## 2. AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)

### 2.1 Relationship to AAWSAP

**AAWSAP and AATIP are not the same program.** This is the single most important factual clarification in the entire subject area.

- **AAWSAP** was the official, funded ($22M) DIA program with a contract (HHM402-08-C-0072) running 2008-2012.
- **AATIP** was an **informal, unfunded** effort that continued after AAWSAP's termination. It operated as a loose community of interest within the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD(I&S)), with no dedicated budget, no formal charter, and no contract.

The **December 2017 *New York Times* story** conflated these two programs, reporting that Luis Elizondo "ran" a program that received $22 million. The $22 million went to AAWSAP under Lacatski's management; Elizondo's AATIP had no dedicated funding.

DIA itself contributed to the confusion. In a November 2009 memo, DIA noted that Reid referred to AAWSAP as "AATIP," suggesting the names were used interchangeably at the political level even before the formal program ended.

**Source Tier: ACADEMIC** -- "On the AAWSAP/AATIP Confusion" (ResearchGate, 2024). **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *New York Times* (Dec. 2017, original conflation); *The Black Vault* (ongoing FOIA-based corrections). **GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- DIA memos via FOIA; AARO Historical Record Report Vol. 1 (2024), which states: "After AAWSAP was cancelled, the AATIP moniker was used by some individuals associated with an informal, unofficial UAP community of interest within DoD."

### 2.2 Luis Elizondo's Role -- The Dispute

**Elizondo's claim:** He directed AATIP from approximately 2010 to October 2017, when he resigned from DoD.

**Pentagon's position:** Spokesperson Christopher Sherwood stated: "Mr. Elizondo had no responsibilities with regard to the AATIP program while he worked in OUSDI, up until the time he resigned effective 10/4/2017."

**Supporting evidence for Elizondo:**
- Sen. Harry Reid wrote to NBC News: "I can state as a matter of record Lue Elizondo's involvement and leadership role in this program."
- Multiple former colleagues have confirmed his involvement in UAP-related work within OUSD(I).

**Complicating factors:**
- If AATIP was never a formally chartered program (as AARO's Historical Report suggests), then Elizondo could not have been "director" of something that did not formally exist -- while simultaneously having done the work he describes.
- In May 2021, Elizondo filed a DoD Inspector General complaint alleging professional retaliation and coordinated disinformation by senior Pentagon officials (naming Garry Reid, Susan Gough, Neill Tipton).
- In February 2022, the DoD OIG's Whistleblower Reprisal Investigations directorate found **insufficient evidence** to support the retaliation claim, stating: "The evidence we reviewed does not support your client's reprisal complaint because no action affecting his eligibility for access to classified information was taken or threatened against him."

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- Pentagon spokesperson statements; DoD OIG complaint and finding (via Black Vault FOIA). **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *The Intercept*; *NBC News*; *Black Vault* document archive. **BOOK/MEMOIR** -- *Imminent: Inside the Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs* (Elizondo, Aug. 2024).

### 2.3 AATIP Budget and Institutional Home

- **Budget:** Zero dedicated appropriation. AATIP personnel used their existing billets and security clearances to pursue UAP investigations as ancillary duties.
- **Institutional home:** Loosely within OUSD(I&S). No formal program office, no program element number, no contract vehicle.
- **Materials-analysis authority:** None documented. AATIP had no budget to commission materials analysis and no known custody of physical samples.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- AARO Historical Record Report Vol. 1 (2024). **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *New York Times*; *Politico*.

## 3. SUCCESSOR PROGRAMS

### 3.1 UAPTF (Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Established** | August 4, 2020 |
| **Authorization** | Deputy Secretary of Defense David L. Norquist |
| **Lead Agency** | Department of the Navy, under cognizance of OUSD(I&S) |
| **Home** | Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI) |
| **Mission** | Detect, analyze, and catalog UAPs that could potentially pose a threat to U.S. national security |
| **Terminated** | Superseded by AARO, July 2022 |

**Relationship to AATIP:** Pentagon spokesperson Sue Gough stated the UAPTF was "not a continuation" of AATIP. However, institutional lineage is evident -- both were under OUSD(I&S) cognizance, and some personnel overlapped.

**Materials custody:** No documented materials-analysis program or custody of physical samples under UAPTF.

**Key output:** The June 2021 "Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena" delivered to Congress -- the first official UAP report since Project Blue Book (1969). Of 144 incidents reviewed, exactly one was explained (deflating balloon); the rest remained unresolved.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- Navy press release (navy.mil, Aug. 4, 2020); ODNI Preliminary Assessment (June 2021).

### 3.2 AARO (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Established** | July 15-20, 2022 |
| **Authorization** | Section 1683, FY2022 NDAA; Deputy SecDef directive |
| **Mandate** | Synchronize detection, reporting, collection, analysis, and resolution of UAP across all domains (air, sea, space, transmedium) |
| **First Director** | Dr. Sean M. Kirkpatrick (physicist, July 2022 -- Dec. 2023) |
| **Interim Director** | Tim Phillips (Oct. 2023 -- Aug. 2024) |
| **Current Director** | Dr. Jon T. Kosloski (NSA Research Directorate; appointed Aug. 26, 2024) |

#### AARO Historical Record Report, Volume 1 (March 8, 2024)

This is the most significant official government document on the history of U.S. government UAP involvement. Key findings regarding materials:

1. **No evidence of reverse-engineering programs:** "AARO found no verifiable evidence that any U.S. Government investigation, academic-sponsored research, or official review panel has confirmed that any sighting of a UAP represented extraterrestrial technology."

2. **Materials analysis results:** AARO, working with a "leading science laboratory" (understood to be Oak Ridge National Laboratory, ORNL), analyzed a metallic sample associated with UAP claims:
   - Composition: Primarily magnesium with bismuth present
   - The material was assessed as **terrestrial in origin** and **possibly of USAF origin**
   - The bismuth was "not a pure layer per initial claims"
   - Isotopic ratios were consistent with terrestrial sources

3. **Kona Blue:** A proposed DHS compartmented program that would have continued AAWSAP-type work. It "was never approved." The proposed lines of effort "closely mirrored those conducted by [BAASS] for AAWSAP/AATIP."

4. **Conflation finding:** AARO assessed that a private sector organization's recreation of the metallic sample "was almost certainly conflated with claims that the aerospace industry was attempting to reverse-engineer off-world technology."

#### AARO Volume 2 and FY2025 Annual Report

- Volume 2 of the Historical Record covers November 2023 through April 2024
- The FY2025 Annual Report (released July 2026) covers June 2024 -- May 2025
- 319 new cases received; 114 resolved (238 cases across periods resolved via new satellite-flaring identification capability)
- Total case holdings: 1,870
- One notable maritime-domain case: ~100 airborne UAP and 2 uncrewed surface systems observed by U.S. Navy assets off Virginia coast

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- AARO Historical Record Report Vol. 1 (media.defense.gov, March 2024); AARO FY2025 Annual Report (aaro.mil, July 2026); DoD establishment directive (July 2022).

### 3.3 DoD Inspector General Evaluation

The DoD IG released an evaluation of DoD's UAP handling (report released January 24, 2024):

- Found **absence of a unified strategy** across DoD components
- Identified **fragmented efforts, inconsistent data collection, and potential security risks**
- Made 11 recommendations; as of January 2024, only **one had been fully implemented**

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- DoD OIG report (Jan. 2024).

### 4.3 TTSA-Army CRADA (2019-2024)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Agreement Number** | CRADA 19-15 |
| **Title** | Novel & Emerging Technology Exploitation (NETE) |
| **Parties** | To The Stars Academy of Arts & Science (TTSA) and U.S. Army DEVCOM Ground Vehicle Systems Center (GVSC) |
| **Announced** | October 2019 |

- TTSA has offered "only limited public response" and released no technical data of its own

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- CRADA agreement (released via Black Vault FOIA); Army analysis results. **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *The War Zone / The Drive*; *Defense Daily*; *Black Vault*.

## 5. FINANCIAL ANALYSIS AND ANOMALIES

### 5.1 The $22M Question

The $22 million figure is **confirmed as the total congressional appropriation** for AAWSAP:
- $10M (FY2008 supplemental)
- $12M (FY2010)

**No evidence of additional government funding** for AAWSAP has surfaced through FOIA, congressional testimony, or credible journalism.

### 5.2 Claims of Additional Black-Budget Funding

**Unverified claims:**
- Grusch's 2023 testimony implied the existence of separate, much larger programs for crash retrieval and reverse engineering, but did not identify specific budget lines.
- The UAP Disclosure Act of 2023 (Schumer-Rounds) included an eminent domain provision for "recovered technologies of unknown origin" and "biological evidence of non-human intelligence" -- implying Congressional awareness of at least claims of such materials. However, this provision was **stripped from the final enacted version** of the FY2024 NDAA.

**What was enacted:** The FY2024 NDAA retained provisions establishing a UAP Records Collection at the National Archives, prohibiting destruction of UAP records, and setting a 25-year disclosure timeline. It allocated $20 million for the Records Collection. The independent Review Board was removed.

### 5.3 Bigelow's Personal Investment

Robert Bigelow invested substantial personal funds in UAP/anomalous research independent of government contracts:

- **1995:** Founded the National Institute for Discovery Science (NIDS), funded entirely from personal wealth
- **1996:** Purchased Skinwalker Ranch (reported price ~$200,000); installed NIDS research team
- **1996-2004:** Funded NIDS Skinwalker investigations out of pocket (team of scientists, engineers, investigators)
- **2008-2012:** BAASS received the $22M government contract; Bigelow reportedly invested additional personal funds in BAASS infrastructure and personnel beyond contract reimbursement
- Bigelow built the institutional and scientific infrastructure (personnel, facilities, databases) that made AAWSAP possible

The exact total of Bigelow's personal expenditure has not been publicly disclosed but is estimated in the tens of millions of dollars over 1995-2020.

### 5.4 Audit and Oversight

- **No dedicated GAO audit** of AAWSAP or AATIP has been identified in the public record.
- The DoD IG evaluation (Jan. 2024) addressed AARO and current UAP handling, not retrospective AAWSAP finances.
- Elizondo's 2021 IG complaint addressed personnel retaliation, not financial irregularities.
- The FY2022 NDAA requirement establishing AARO included provisions for Congressional reporting and oversight.

**Assessment:** The $22M AAWSAP contract appears to have been executed through standard DIA contracting channels with normal congressional earmark procedures. The financial anomaly, such as it is, lies in the **procurement process** (single-bidder response to a narrowly scoped solicitation) rather than in hidden spending.

**Source Tier: GOVERNMENT RECORD** -- FY2024 NDAA text; DoD IG report. **CREDENTIALED JOURNALISM** -- *Reason* (Nov. 2022); *The Debrief*; *DefenseScoop*.
