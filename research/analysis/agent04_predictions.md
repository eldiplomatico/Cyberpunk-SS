## 1. METHODOLOGY AND SCORING FRAMEWORK

### 1.1 Specificity Scale

| Rating | Definition | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| VAGUE | No testable parameters; could apply to almost anything | "Something bad will happen someday" |
| LOW | One testable parameter but broadly stated | "An electromagnetic event in the future" |
| MODERATE | Multiple parameters but missing key specifics | "The US has recovered alien craft since 1947" |
| SPECIFIC | Contains falsifiable details (dates, quantities, locations, mechanisms) | "More than 10 craft recovered by the US" |
| PRECISE | Fully testable with named objects, dates, and measurable outcomes | (None found in this investigation) |

### 1.2 Testability Assessment

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| TESTABLE | Could be confirmed or refuted with available or obtainable evidence |
| PARTIALLY TESTABLE | Some elements could be checked, others cannot |
| UNTESTABLE | Structured to resist falsification (classified, subjective, unfalsifiable timeline) |
| TESTED | Has been subjected to verification attempts |

### 1.3 Retrodiction Scale (1-10)

Measures whether a prediction could have been generated from publicly available information at the time it was made.

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 1-2 | Prediction contains information clearly NOT available publicly at time of claim |
| 3-4 | Prediction contains elements that were obscure but theoretically accessible |
| 5-6 | Prediction is consistent with generally available knowledge at the time |
| 7-8 | Prediction closely tracks publicly known information or trends |
| 9-10 | Prediction is essentially a restatement of publicly available information |

### 1.4 Source Tier Key (from Wave 1 reports)

- **Tier 1:** FOIA documents, peer-reviewed publications, government records, court filings
- **Tier 2:** Major investigative journalism, official statements, firsthand conference presentations
- **Tier 3:** Books by participants, secondary journalism, journals of questionable editorial standards
- **Tier 4:** Podcasts, social media, documentary interviews
- **Tier 5:** Thirdhand accounts, viral social media, anonymous sources

## 13. ANALYTICAL SUMMARY

### 13.1 Key Findings

**Finding 1: No prediction in this dataset contains information demonstrably unavailable to the public at the time it was made.**

Of 47 extracted claims, zero score below 3 on the retrodiction scale. The closest candidates (Puthoff's AAWSAP disclosures, the ZPE/inertia paper, the Jupiter rings claim) are either (a) insider knowledge rather than foreknowledge, (b) theoretical contributions rather than predictions, or (c) subject to documentation disputes.

**Finding 2: Multiple catastrophe predictions exist, but they predict DIFFERENT catastrophes through DIFFERENT mechanisms with DIFFERENT timelines.**

- Sherman: EM collapse, no date
- Eskridge/Puthoff attr.: Apocalypse with survivors, "near future"
- Farsight: Oceanic meteor impact, "before 2013" (failed), "2020-2025" (failed)
- RV community: Unspecified 2027 events

The appearance of convergence dissolves upon examination. These are not multiple sources confirming the same prediction -- they are separate, contradictory predictions that have been retrospectively grouped under a "catastrophe" umbrella.

**Finding 3: The Puthoff-Davis-Green-Bigelow network is the common node in nearly all UAP/NHI claims.**

When Puthoff, Grusch, Elizondo, and other disclosure advocates make similar claims, this does not constitute independent corroboration because they all operate within the same interconnected network (documented in Agent 11). Claims that appear to come from multiple independent sources frequently trace back to 5-10 interconnected individuals.

**Finding 4: The catastrophe claim attributed to Puthoff (via Eskridge) has the weakest provenance of any major claim in the dataset.**

- Sole source: Amy Eskridge (deceased)
- Unconfirmed by Puthoff
- Uses terminology (P-47/P-52) from a separate conspiracy tradition (Dan Burisch)

- The narrative has demonstrably mutated through retelling
- Agent 02 assessment: "VERY LOW CONFIDENCE"

**Finding 5: Astronomical evidence provides zero support for any catastrophe prediction in the 2025-2040 window.**

Agent 05 established:
- All cataloged asteroids rate Torino Scale 0 for the next 100 years
- No known object has meaningful impact probability in 2025-2040
- No catastrophe prediction examined names a specific object, date, or provides orbital parameters
- Agent 06 found no hidden deflection payloads on any spacecraft

**Finding 6: The "missing scientists" narrative does not constitute evidence for the catastrophe predictions.**

Agent 08 established:
- 7+ of 14 cases have clear ordinary explanations (solved homicides, debunked disappearances, non-scientists)
- Only 1 of 14 (Eskridge) has any claimed connection to Puthoff, and that claim is unverified
- The narrative was constructed backward (deaths first, then searching for connections)
- Statistical analysis shows the death rate is indistinguishable from base rates

**Finding 7: The two verified predictions in the dataset are both failures.**

The only predictions with specific enough timelines to test against deadlines are:
1. Farsight "before 2013" meteor impact: **FAILED**
2. Farsight "2020-2025" comet swarm: **FAILED**

All other predictions either (a) have no timeline, (b) have not yet reached their alleged window, or (c) are structured to be permanently unfalsifiable.

### 13.4 Bottom Line

The prediction database reveals a landscape of claims that share three common characteristics:

1. **Unfalsifiability** -- Most are structured to resist testing (no dates, no specifics, classified sources)
2. **Network dependency** -- Most trace to the same small group of interconnected individuals
3. **Cultural derivation** -- Most contain information readily available from public sources, science fiction, or pre-existing UFO folklore

The appearance of a convergent "signal" pointing toward an impending catastrophe is, upon systematic analysis, an artifact of:
- Retrospective thematic grouping of unrelated predictions
- Network reinforcement mistaken for independent corroboration
- Narrative mutation through social media amplification
- Base rate neglect in evaluating scientist deaths
- Confirmation bias in selecting which claims to amplify

This does not prove that all underlying claims are false. It demonstrates that the evidential chain connecting publicly available predictions to a specific catastrophic threat is, at present, broken at every link.
