### Current Survey Completeness

|---|---|---|---|---|
| > 1 km diameter | > 90% | ~1,000 NEAs | ~1,100 found | ~0-100 remaining |

### Probability Assessment: Could an Undetected Threatening Object Exist?

**For objects > 1 km (civilization-threatening):**
- Survey completeness > 90%
- Remaining unknown: ~0-100 objects
- Annual impact probability per unknown object: ~1 in 500,000
- Combined probability of impact from an undiscovered > 1 km object in any given decade: approximately 0.0002% to 0.002% (1 in 50,000 to 1 in 500,000)
- **Assessment: EXTREMELY LOW.** The overwhelming majority of large NEAs are known and tracked. None are on impact trajectories.

**For objects 140 m - 1 km (regional catastrophe):**
- Survey completeness: ~38%
- Remaining unknown: ~13,600 objects
- Annual impact probability per object in this size range: ~1 in 10,000 to 1 in 20,000
- Combined probability of a surprise impact from this population in any given decade: approximately 0.05% to 0.1% (1 in 1,000 to 1 in 2,000)
- **Assessment: VERY LOW but not negligible over multi-decade timescales.** This is the primary capability gap that NEO Surveyor (launch 2027) and Vera Rubin Observatory are designed to close.

**For objects 50-140 m (city-destroyer, Tunguska-class):**
- Survey completeness: < 10%
- Remaining unknown: ~180,000+
- These objects are numerous but impacts are rare (mean interval ~500-1,000 years for Tunguska-class)
- Probability of surprise impact in any given decade: approximately 1% to 2%
- **Assessment: LOW but the highest probability category for an unwarned event.** However, a 50 m impactor, while devastating locally, would not constitute the "apocalypse" or "extinction" events described in the claims.

**For long-period comets (the most dangerous gap):**
- Long-period comets from the Oort cloud are the most significant blind spot
- They arrive on hyperbolic or near-parabolic orbits and are detectable only months to a few years before closest approach
- Known near-Earth comets: ~130
- The population of undiscovered long-period comets is essentially unbounded
- However, the historical impact rate from long-period comets is much lower than from NEAs
- Probability of a large (> 1 km) cometary impact in any given century: roughly 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 100,000
- **Assessment: VERY LOW but represents the irreducible uncertainty in impact hazard assessment.**

### Key Conclusion on "Undiscovered Object" Scenario

An undiscovered threatening object COULD theoretically exist, but:
1. The probability is extremely low for civilization-threatening objects (> 1 km)
2. None of the predictions examined contain any information suggesting knowledge of such an object (no orbital elements, no size, no trajectory, no composition, no approach direction)
3. The vagueness of the predictions is precisely calibrated to exploit the residual uncertainty in survey completeness -- they can always point to "what we haven't found yet" as a gap their prediction might fill
4. This is an unfalsifiable epistemic structure: "There might be something out there we haven't found" is always true, but it is not evidence for any specific prediction

## 6. THE EM CATASTROPHE SCENARIO (SHERMAN) VS. SOLAR PHYSICS

### Sherman's Prediction

Sherman claims a future event will "wipe out all electromagnetic energy" worldwide. He does not specify a cause, but the most scientifically plausible interpretation is a severe solar event -- specifically a Carrington-class or stronger coronal mass ejection (CME) or solar proton event.

### Solar Physics: CME/Carrington Event Probabilities

**Historical Precedent:**
- **1859 Carrington Event:** The most intense geomagnetic storm on record. Auroras visible at tropical latitudes. Telegraph systems worldwide failed, with some operators receiving electric shocks. This occurred during Solar Cycle 10.
- **1921 New York Railroad Storm:** Major geomagnetic storm causing widespread telegraph and newly-installed telephone disruptions.
- **July 23, 2012 Near-Miss:** A Carrington-class CME was ejected from the Sun in Earth's orbital path, but Earth had already moved past that point ~9 days earlier. Had it occurred one week earlier, it would have struck Earth directly. This event was detected by the STEREO-A spacecraft.
- **May 2024 Geomagnetic Storm (G5):** The strongest storm since 2003, caused by multiple CMEs. Produced widespread aurora but limited infrastructure damage due to modern grid hardening.

**Probability Estimates (from peer-reviewed literature):**

| Event Class | Estimated Probability per Decade | Key Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Carrington-class event (Dst < -850 nT) | ~1.6% to 12% | Riley 2012, Space Weather; Kataoka 2013; Love 2021 |
| Extreme event (Dst < -500 nT) | ~5% to 15% | Multiple sources |
| "Super-Carrington" event (10x Carrington) | < 0.1% per decade | Theoretical estimates |

**Key findings from solar physics:**
1. A Carrington-class event striking Earth is a statistical certainty over century timescales -- the question is when, not if.
2. The probability in any given decade is roughly 1-12%, depending on methodology and the definition of "Carrington-class."
3. The July 2012 near-miss demonstrates that such events DO occur at regular intervals; Earth simply was not in the path.
4. Solar Cycle 25 is expected to peak around 2024-2025 and decline through 2030. CME risk is somewhat elevated during solar maximum but extreme events can occur at any phase.

### Would a Carrington-Class Event "Wipe Out All Electromagnetic Energy"?

**No, but the effects would be severe:**
- Widespread power grid failures, potentially lasting weeks to months in worst-case scenarios
- Satellite damage and GPS disruption
- Radio communication blackouts
- Damage to unshielded electronics
- Estimated economic damage: $0.6 to $2.6 trillion (NAS 2008 estimate)

**What a CME would NOT do:**
- Permanently eliminate all electromagnetic phenomena (electromagnetism is a fundamental force of nature, not a technology)
- Destroy all electronics simultaneously (shielded, underground, and off-grid systems would survive)
- Cause an "extinction" event
- Affect fundamental physics

### Assessment of Sherman's EM Catastrophe Prediction

|---|---|

| Is the probability high enough that predicting it is meaningful? | MARGINALLY. A ~1-12% per decade probability means this is a real risk but not an imminent certainty. Predicting "a severe solar storm will happen eventually" is trivially true and not evidence of insider knowledge. |
