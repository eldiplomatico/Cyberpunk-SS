## 6. THE PUTHOFF-CATASTROPHE CLAIM (Full Provenance Investigation)

### The Claim as Currently Circulating
"Hal Puthoff told Amy Eskridge about a coming catastrophe" -- specifically, that ultraterrestrials are humans from the future (designated P-47 and P-52) who have traveled back in time following an apocalyptic event that decimated humanity.

### A. WHETHER THE CONVERSATION IS DOCUMENTED ANYWHERE

**PRIMARY SOURCE:** The May 20, 2020 video interview of Amy Eskridge by Jeremy Rys and Mark Sokol. This is the ONLY known primary source.

In this recorded interview, Amy Eskridge states (paraphrased from multiple secondary reports of the video content):
- "Hal talks about ultraterrestrials, they're us from the future"
- "There's an apocalypse scenario in the near future" that "wipes out most everything"
- Some people "go underground to survive" while others "miraculously stay on the surface and don't die"
- P-47 = "Present + 47,000 years" (one evolutionary branch of post-catastrophe future humanity)
- P-52 = "Present + 52,000 years" (a more technologically/spiritually advanced branch)
- Hal Puthoff "came over to her house multiple times with an investor" and "spoke about Ultraterrestrials"
- These visits were "presumably" to talk to her father Richard Eskridge

She also stated in the same interview or related recordings:
- "Hal is actually extremely evil"
- "Lue [Elizondo] is evil"
- "Brandenburg is extremely evil"

**CRITICAL NOTE:** Amy is reporting what she says Puthoff said. She is a **secondhand source** at best. The conversation she describes between Puthoff and her father (or Puthoff and herself) has no corroboration from any other participant.

### B. WHO FIRST MADE THIS CLAIM PUBLICLY

**Amy Eskridge herself** made this claim publicly in the May 20, 2020 video interview with Jeremy Rys and Mark Sokol.

The claim was then **amplified** by:
1. UFO/UAP community members who shared clips from the interview (various dates, 2022-2025)
2. Ashton Forbes on X/Twitter (earliest identified tweet: January 2024, tweet ID 1750955143770632449)
3. Red Panda Koala archived the interview on Internet Archive
4. Mainstream media picked it up during the "missing scientists" panic of April 2026
5. IBTimes UK ran specific coverage of the P-47/P-52 claims in context of Pentagon UAP file releases (2026)

### C. WHEN THE CLAIM FIRST APPEARED PUBLICLY

**May 20, 2020** -- in the recorded video interview. This is approximately 2 years before her death and approximately 2 years before Puthoff published his "Ultraterrestrial Models" paper (July 2022).

The P-47/P-52 terminology she uses does NOT come from Puthoff's published paper. It originates from a different strand of UFO lore associated with **Dan Burisch and the alleged "Majestic 12" timeline files**, including claims connected to "Project Looking Glass" (a purported classified program for viewing probabilistic futures). This is a pre-existing fringe narrative that Eskridge mapped onto her account of Puthoff's statements.

### D. WHETHER THE ALLEGED CONVERSATION WAS RECORDED

**No.** There is no recording of the conversation between Puthoff and the Eskridges. The only recording is of Amy Eskridge **describing** the alleged conversation to her interviewers in May 2020.

### E. WHETHER THERE ARE WITNESSES

**Unknown.** Amy states Puthoff came with "an investor" (unnamed) and "presumably" spoke to her father Richard Eskridge. However:
- Richard Eskridge has not publicly confirmed or described these visits
- Richard Eskridge has actively rejected conspiracy theories about his daughter
- The unnamed "investor" has never been identified
- No other family member or associate has corroborated the visits

### F. WHETHER ESKRIDGE HERSELF DOCUMENTED IT

**Partially.** She discussed it in the May 2020 video interview. No writings, emails, social media posts (including from her pseudonymous accounts RandoCalrissian or Drunk Ufo Insider), or other documentation of the alleged Puthoff conversations have surfaced.

### G. WHETHER PUTHOFF HAS EVER CONFIRMED OR DENIED IT

**No public confirmation or denial found.** Hal Puthoff has published on the ultraterrestrial hypothesis (his "Ultraterrestrial Models" paper appeared July 30, 2022, in the Journal of Cosmology, after Eskridge's death on June 11, 2022), but:
- The paper discusses various ultraterrestrial hypotheses in academic terms
- The paper does NOT use the P-47/P-52 terminology
- The paper does NOT describe a specific catastrophe scenario
- Puthoff has not publicly commented on Amy Eskridge's claims about him
- Puthoff has not confirmed visiting the Eskridge home

### H. WHETHER THE STORY CHANGED OVER TIME (Narrative Evolution)

**YES -- significant evolution detected:**

| Stage | Timeframe | Version of the Story |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| ORIGINAL | May 2020 (Eskridge interview) | Puthoff visited her house multiple times with an investor; spoke about ultraterrestrials as "us from the future"; there's an apocalypse scenario; she also called Puthoff "evil" |
| EARLY RETELLING | 2022-2024 (UFO community) | Eskridge "knew about ultraterrestrials from Puthoff"; the "evil" characterization sometimes dropped |
| AMPLIFIED | Jan 2024+ (Ashton Forbes et al.) | Framed as "silenced scientist who knew the truth about ultraterrestrials"; Forbes incorrectly names father as "Greg"; the nuance of Amy's ambivalent/hostile relationship with Puthoff (calling him "evil") is largely erased |
| VIRAL | April-July 2026 | "Puthoff told Eskridge about a coming catastrophe" becomes a standalone factoid; the P-47/P-52 lore (which comes from Dan Burisch, not Puthoff) is conflated with Puthoff's published ultraterrestrial paper; her death is presented as assassination for knowing this "truth" |

**Key distortions in the narrative evolution:**
1. The P-47/P-52 framework is attributed to Puthoff, but it actually comes from the Dan Burisch/Majestic 12/Project Looking Glass lore -- a completely separate and much older strand of UFO conspiracy culture
2. Eskridge's hostile characterization of Puthoff as "evil" is increasingly omitted in retellings that frame her as a victim of silencing for possessing Puthoff-derived knowledge
3. The "coming catastrophe" becomes more specific and urgent in each retelling, though in the original interview it is vague
4. Her father's documented rejection of conspiracy theories about her death is almost never mentioned in pro-conspiracy retellings
5. Her lack of peer-reviewed publications is obscured by inflated credentials (the false "Dr." title)

## 11. ASSESSMENT AND CONFIDENCE LEVEL

### On Amy Eskridge as a Person
**HIGH CONFIDENCE:** Amy Catherine Eskridge (1987-2022) was a real person from Huntsville, Alabama, with a genuine interest in advanced propulsion and anti-gravity research. She had a double major in Chemistry and Biology from UAH. She co-founded the Institute for Exotic Science and HoloChron Engineering with her father Richard Eskridge, who was a legitimate NASA Marshall Space Flight Center propulsion researcher. She presented publicly on anti-gravity topics and was active in online UFO/UAP communities. She died on June 11, 2022, from a gunshot wound officially ruled a suicide.

### On the Puthoff Visits
**LOW CONFIDENCE:** There is no independent corroboration that Hal Puthoff visited the Eskridge home. The claim rests solely on Amy's statements in a single recorded interview. Puthoff has not confirmed or denied this. Richard Eskridge has not confirmed this. The unnamed "investor" has never been identified. It is plausible given overlapping professional interests (both families involved in advanced physics/propulsion) but unverified.

### On "Puthoff Told Eskridge About a Coming Catastrophe"
**VERY LOW CONFIDENCE:** This specific claim as commonly circulated is a multi-layered distortion:
1. The "catastrophe/apocalypse" described by Amy uses the P-47/P-52 framework, which originates from Dan Burisch/Majestic 12 conspiracy lore, NOT from Puthoff's own published work
2. Amy is reporting secondhand what she claims Puthoff said; there is no direct documentation
3. Amy simultaneously called Puthoff "evil" -- a critical context routinely omitted
4. Puthoff's own published paper on ultraterrestrials (July 2022) does not contain these specific claims
5. No other person has corroborated the conversations
6. The narrative has demonstrably mutated through retelling, becoming more specific and dramatic over time

### On Her Death
**MODERATE CONFIDENCE in official ruling:** The official suicide ruling is supported by:
- Family acceptance
- Known chronic pain condition (nortriptyline prescription)
- Madison County Coroner determination
- Local police investigation (though limited details are public)

Factors creating uncertainty:
- Pre-death text stating "I did not kill myself"
- Alleged directed energy weapon targeting claims
- No public autopsy report, police report, or detailed investigation results
- Her case is now part of a federal examination alongside other deaths

The pre-death text is the strongest single piece of evidence against the official ruling, but such statements can also be consistent with paranoid ideation associated with mental health crises. The text alone does not constitute evidence of foul play.

### On the Broader "Missing Scientists" Connection
**VERY LOW CONFIDENCE:** The aggregation of 11 disparate cases spanning multiple years, locations, and circumstances into a single conspiracy narrative exhibits classic apophenia. The cases involve different causes of death (suicide, homicide, natural causes, disappearance), different research areas, different employers, and different geographic locations. No evidence has been publicly presented demonstrating coordination or connection between the cases. The FBI examination was announced in response to public and political pressure rather than specific investigative findings linking the cases.

## ANALYTICAL SUMMARY

The claim "Hal Puthoff told Amy Eskridge about a coming catastrophe" traces to a single source: Amy Eskridge's own statements in a May 2020 video interview. It has been amplified through a chain of social media accounts, journalists, and viral content, but no independent verification has emerged at any point in this chain. The specific "catastrophe" framework (P-47/P-52 designations) that Eskridge used does not originate with Puthoff but with a separate, older strand of UFO conspiracy lore (Dan Burisch/Majestic 12). Puthoff has neither confirmed nor denied the claim. The family most able to corroborate it has rejected the broader conspiracy narrative. The claim has demonstrably mutated through retelling, growing more specific and dramatic while shedding context (such as Eskridge simultaneously calling Puthoff "evil") that complicates simple narratives.

This is a case study in how a single unverified claim by one individual can, through social media dynamics and narrative aggregation with unrelated events, become widely accepted as established fact despite having no independent corroboration whatsoever.
