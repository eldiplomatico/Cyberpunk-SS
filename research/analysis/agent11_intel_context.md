## 2. SRI REMOTE VIEWING PROGRAM -- FACTS

### 2.1 Origins and Structure

The CIA-initiated remote viewing program at Stanford Research Institute (SRI, later SRI International) began in 1972. The program was founded and first directed by **Harold E. Puthoff, PhD** (1972-1985), a theoretical and experimental physicist who had previously worked at the NSA, General Electric, and Sperry before joining Stanford University where he earned his PhD in 1967.

Puthoff's co-principal investigator was physicist **Russell Targ**. Together they led government-funded research into extrasensory perception (ESP) at SRI, tasked with determining whether parapsychological phenomena, including remote viewing, could be scientifically validated and operationally exploited for intelligence collection.

**Edwin May** later succeeded Puthoff as program director (1985-1995).

### 2.2 Program Code Names (Chronological)

The program operated under multiple code names as it moved between sponsoring agencies:

1. **Project Scanate** (1972-1977) -- CIA-sponsored initial research at SRI
2. **Gondola Wish** -- Early Army involvement
3. **Grill Flame** (1978/79-1983) -- Integrated SRI research under DIA/Army INSCOM
4. **INSCOM Center Lane Project (ICLP)** (1983-1985) -- Army INSCOM designation
5. **Sun Streak** (late 1985-1991) -- After Army funding terminated, redesignated under DIA's Scientific and Technical Intelligence Directorate
6. **Star Gate** (1991-1995) -- Consolidated program name under DIA

### 2.3 Scale and Duration

- **Duration:** 23 years (1972-1995)
- **Total funding:** Estimated $20+ million over the life of the program
- **Sponsoring agencies:** CIA, DIA, Army Intelligence and Security Command (INSCOM), Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA)
- **FOIA release:** In July 1995, CIA declassified and approved for release documents revealing the program. The CIA FOIA system ultimately released approximately **12,000 files totaling 89,000 pages** of material from the program's entire history.

### 2.4 Key Subjects/Remote Viewers

Notable remote viewers who participated include Pat Price, Ingo Swann, Joe McMoneagle, and others. Christopher "Kit" Green of the CIA's Office of Scientific Intelligence was documented as a key intelligence community liaison.

### 2.5 The 1995 AIR Evaluation

In 1995, the CIA contracted the **American Institutes for Research (AIR)** to conduct an independent evaluation of the Stargate program. The final report was released September 29, 1995, and made public November 28, 1995.

**Split conclusions from two principal evaluators:**

- **Jessica Utts (statistician):** Argued the statistical evidence for remote viewing was strong enough to reject the null hypothesis. The effect sizes were consistent and replicable.
- **Ray Hyman (psychologist):** Disagreed, arguing methodological flaws in experimental design made results scientifically unreliable.

**Critical agreement:** Both evaluators agreed that even if remote viewing was real, the intelligence it produced was **too vague and inconsistent to be operationally useful** in the field.

**Outcome:** The CIA accepted Hyman's more skeptical conclusions and officially terminated the program.

### 2.6 What Happened After Termination

After Stargate's official termination in 1995:
- Puthoff had already departed SRI in 1985 to found the **Institute for Advanced Studies at Austin** (later incorporated as **EarthTech International, Inc.** in 1991)
- Several former program participants continued remote viewing research in private capacities
- Puthoff continued research into quantum vacuum physics, gravity, and advanced propulsion concepts
- The declassification generated significant public interest and a wave of books by former participants
- No documented evidence of continued government remote viewing programs exists in the public record, though allegations persist

**SOURCE:** CIA FOIA Reading Room (foia.cia.gov); AIR 1995 evaluation report (CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180005-5); Federation of American Scientists (irp.fas.org/program/collect/stargate.htm)

## 3. AAWSAP / AATIP -- DOCUMENTED RECORD

### 3.1 AAWSAP: The Original Program

**Full name:** Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program
**Sponsoring agency:** Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)
**Duration:** 2007-2012
**Funding:** $22 million (appropriated through classified "black budget")
**Contract recipient:** Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), a subsidiary of Robert Bigelow's Bigelow Aerospace

**Congressional sponsors:** The program was initiated by three senior senators:
- **Harry Reid** (D-NV) -- Senate Majority Leader
- **Ted Stevens** (R-AK) -- Senate Appropriations Committee
- **Daniel Inouye** (D-HI) -- Senate Appropriations Committee

These three senators inserted a provision into the 2008 Supplemental Appropriations Bill directing funds to a five-year study within the Defense Department. The funding was placed in the "dark budget" and was not known to the public or the rest of Congress until 2017.

### 3.2 AAWSAP Activities

Under the DIA contract, BAASS conducted:
- Investigation of Skinwalker Ranch (Bigelow had purchased the property in 1996)
- Study of UAP reports and incidents
- Investigation of anomalous phenomena reported by military personnel
- Production of 38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs) -- technical research papers

The program was led by **James Lacatski** (DIA program manager) with assistance from **Jay Stratton** and others. BAASS operated with approximately 75 personnel.

**Puthoff's role:** Served as **chief scientist for BAASS** and **chief science advisor to AAWSAP**. He authored at least one DIRD ("Advanced Space Propulsion Based on Vacuum (Spacetime Metric) Engineering").

### 3.3 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents (DIRDs)

The AAWSAP program produced **38 DIRDs** covering advanced physics and technology topics. On March 25, 2022, the DIA completed a FOIA request and released 37 of the 38 reports. Topics included:

- Warp Drive, Dark Energy, and the Manipulation of Extra Dimensions
- Traversable Wormholes, Stargates, and Negative Energy
- Antigravity for Aerospace Applications
- Concepts for Extracting Energy from the Quantum Vacuum
- Advanced Space Propulsion Based on Vacuum Engineering
- Invisibility Cloaking
- High-Frequency Gravitational Wave Communications
- Metamaterials for Aerospace Use
- Bioeffects of electromagnetic radiation
- High-Energy Laser Weapons
- Quantum Computing and Sensors

**Key authors:** Eric Davis, Hal Puthoff, and various contracted physicists and engineers.

**Assessment of DIRDs:** These were largely literature reviews and theoretical explorations of speculative physics concepts. They represent the state of theoretical knowledge on these topics, not evidence of operational technologies. Many topics are at Technology Readiness Level 1-2 (basic principles observed / concept formulated).

### 3.4 AATIP: The Successor / Parallel Program

**Full name:** Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program
**Duration:** Approximately 2010-2017
**Location:** Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (OUSD(I))

The relationship between AAWSAP and AATIP is documented but contested:
- In 2011, the DIA's AAWSAP contract ended/funding expired
- The program was apparently continued in some form under the AATIP designation within the Pentagon's counterintelligence portfolio
- **Luis Elizondo** claims he ran AATIP from approximately 2010 until his resignation in October 2017

**Contested claims about Elizondo's role:**
- In 2017, Pentagon spokeswoman Dana White confirmed Elizondo as an AATIP leader to Politico
- In 2019, Pentagon spokesman Christopher Sherwood stated Elizondo "had no responsibilities with regard to the AATIP program while he worked in OUSDI"
- This contradiction has never been officially resolved

### 3.5 The December 2017 New York Times Story

On December 16, 2017, the New York Times published a front-page story revealing:
- The existence of AATIP
- $22 million in Pentagon funding
- Three Navy UAP videos (FLIR1/Gimbal/GoFast)
- Elizondo's claims about the program

This story brought UAP back into mainstream discourse and triggered subsequent congressional action.

**SOURCE:** DIA FOIA releases; NYT 12/16/2017; Congressional appropriations records; 8 News Now (Las Vegas) investigative reports; The Debrief; Vice News FOIA reporting

## 4. PLANETARY DEFENSE -- GOVERNMENT PROGRAMS

### 4.1 NASA Planetary Defense Coordination Office (PDCO)

**Established:** January 2016
**Agency:** NASA
**Mandate:** Manage NASA's ongoing mission of finding, tracking, characterizing, and cataloging near-Earth objects (NEOs) that could pose an impact hazard to Earth.

Key functions:
- Early detection of Potentially Hazardous Objects (PHOs) -- NEOs whose orbits bring them within 5 million miles of Earth's orbit and large enough (30-50 meters+) to cause significant damage
- Tracking and characterization of known NEOs
- Coordinating U.S. government response planning
- Congressional mandate: find, track, and characterize at least 90% of NEOs 140 meters and larger

### 4.2 Detection and Assessment Systems

- **CNEOS (Center for Near Earth Object Studies)** at JPL: hosts the Sentry System (scans asteroid catalogs, estimates impact probability over 100 years) and Scout System (monitors newly detected objects)
- **NEO Surveyor mission:** Space-based infrared telescope designed to accelerate NEO discovery
- **PDCO serves as official conduit for all asteroid-related information in the United States**

### 4.3 National Strategy and Policy

- **National Near-Earth Object Preparedness Strategy and Action Plan** (2023 update, originally 2018 under Trump administration)
- **NEO Impact Threat Emergency Protocols** (January 2021, published by White House Office of Science and Technology Policy)
- These documents are publicly available and outline interagency coordination for NEO threats

### 4.4 FEMA-NASA Planetary Defense Exercises

NASA and FEMA have conducted regular interagency tabletop exercises simulating asteroid impact scenarios:

| Exercise | Date | Location | Key Features |
|----------|------|----------|-------------|
| TTX 1 | 2013 | -- | Initial concept |
| TTX 2 | 2014 | -- | Expanded scenario |
| TTX 3 | 2016 | -- | International participation |
| TTX 4 | Feb 2022 | Johns Hopkins APL, Laurel, MD | Simulated "Asteroid 2022 TTX" atmospheric entry |
| TTX 5 | Apr 2024 | Johns Hopkins APL, Laurel, MD | First to use DART mission data; international leaders participated |

These exercises are publicly documented and represent the open, civilian-led approach to planetary defense.

### 4.5 NASA DART Mission

The **Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART)** mission impacted asteroid moonlet **Dimorphos** on September 26, 2022, successfully demonstrating kinetic impactor technology for planetary defense. This was the first in-space demonstration of a technology for defending Earth against potential asteroid impacts.

**SOURCE:** NASA PDCO (science.nasa.gov); JPL CNEOS; White House OSTP NEO protocols; Johns Hopkins APL press releases; NASA DART mission documentation

## 5. CLASSIFICATION POLICIES (What Gets Classified and Why)

### 5.1 Military Sensor Data on Bolides/Fireballs

**Key finding:** The U.S. military **did** classify asteroid/bolide detection data for decades.

- U.S. government sensors (military early-warning satellites designed to detect missile launches and nuclear detonations) also detect atmospheric impact events from asteroids and meteoroids
- This bolide data was classified because it was collected by classified sensor systems -- the data itself was not sensitive, but the **sensor capabilities were**
- Bolide light curve data (brightness changes as objects enter atmosphere) was restricted

**2022 Declassification:** In April 2022, an agreement between NASA and the U.S. Space Force authorized public release of decades of bolide data going back to 1988 for nearly 1,000 bolide events. This was explicitly for the benefit of the planetary defense community.

**Key distinction:** The classification was about **protecting sensor capabilities**, not about hiding asteroid threats. The orbital parameters and impact locations were generally shared with NASA/CNEOS even when the detailed sensor data was restricted. There is no documented evidence of the government withholding information about a genuine impact threat to Earth.

### 5.2 UAP Classification

UAP-related military reports have been classified on the basis of:
- **Sources and methods:** Sensor capabilities, intelligence collection platforms
- **National security:** Potential adversary technology assessment
- **Operational security:** Military asset locations and capabilities

The AARO FY2024 report noted that of 1,652 total UAP reports documented, 757 were received during the May 2023-June 2024 reporting period. AARO resolved 49 cases during the period, all to prosaic explanations (balloons, birds, UAS). An additional 243 cases were recommended for closure, also resolved to prosaic objects.

### 5.3 Remote Viewing Program Classification

The Stargate program was classified at the SECRET level, primarily to protect:
- Intelligence sources and methods
- Operational tasking details
- Relationships with human intelligence assets
- The existence of the program itself (to avoid public embarrassment and adversary awareness)

The 1995 declassification released the vast majority of program documentation.

## 7. ALLEGED BUT UNCONFIRMED PROGRAMS

The following are claims that appear in public discourse but **lack TIER 1 verification** (no FOIA releases, no official government confirmation, no congressional records):

### 7.1 Wilson-Davis Memo

**Claim:** A 15-page document allegedly recording an April 9-10, 2002 meeting between Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson (recently retired DIA Director) and astrophysicist Eric Davis. In it, Wilson purportedly describes his 1997 attempt to gain access to a classified special access program (SAP) involving crash retrieval of a non-human craft, and being denied access by the private contractor running the program.

**Status:**
- Document leaked from Edgar Mitchell's estate after his 2016 death
- Admiral Wilson has denied ever meeting Eric Davis or sharing the information
- Davis has neither confirmed nor denied the memo's authenticity (citing security clearances)
- The Pentagon has neither confirmed nor dismissed the document
- **Source tier: TIER 4** -- leaked document of disputed authenticity; principal denies it

### 7.2 Crash Retrieval / Reverse Engineering Programs

**Claim (Grusch):** In June-July 2023, David Grusch (former intelligence officer, NGA/NRO background) publicly claimed:
- The US government maintains a highly secretive UFO recovery program
- The government possesses "non-human" spacecraft
- "Dead pilots" have been recovered
- The US has been aware of "non-human" activity since the 1930s

**Status:**
- Grusch filed a whistleblower complaint with the IC Inspector General
- IC IG found his complaint "credible and urgent" (this means the complaint met procedural thresholds for investigation, not that the IG verified the underlying claims)
- Grusch testified under oath before Congress (July 26, 2023)
- Grusch stated his claims are based on information from other individuals, not firsthand knowledge
- AARO Historical Record Report (March 2024) found "no empirical evidence" supporting these claims
- AARO interviewed relevant personnel and company executives who denied possessing off-world technology "on the record"
- **Source tier: TIER 2** (sworn congressional testimony is credible sourcing) **for the existence of the claims**; **TIER 4** for the underlying allegations themselves (secondhand, unverified)

### 7.3 Continuation of Remote Viewing Programs

**Claim:** Remote viewing programs continued after the 1995 Stargate termination under different code names or within private contractors.

**Status:**
- No FOIA documents confirm post-1995 government remote viewing programs
- Several former participants continued private remote viewing activities
- Some AAWSAP/BAASS activities reportedly included consciousness-related research
- **Source tier: TIER 3-4** -- participant claims, no government documentation

### 7.4 "The Invisible College" / Informal Advisory Network

**Claim:** An informal network of scientists and intelligence officers has maintained continuous interest in UAP phenomena across decades, operating partially outside official channels.

**Status:**
- Partially documented: The Puthoff-Davis-Green-Vallee-Bigelow network is real and their advisory roles to multiple programs are documented
- The term "invisible college" was used by Jacques Vallee and is acknowledged in various published works
- What is disputed is whether this network has access to hidden programs or classified information beyond what is documented

## 8. WHAT DECLASSIFIED DOCUMENTS ACTUALLY SHOW

### 8.1 CIA Stargate FOIA Release (1995)

**Volume:** ~12,000 files, ~89,000 pages
**What they show:**
- A real, funded, 23-year program investigating remote viewing
- Mixed experimental results that statisticians disagreed about
- Operational tasking of remote viewers against intelligence targets
- Program was taken seriously enough to fund for over two decades
- Intelligence produced was generally too vague for operational use
- No evidence of confirmed extraterrestrial contact through remote viewing

### 8.2 DIA AAWSAP DIRD Release (2022)

**Volume:** 37 of 38 technical reports released
**What they show:**
- The US intelligence community funded speculative physics research
- Topics ranged from warp drives to quantum vacuum energy to invisibility cloaking
- These were largely literature reviews and theoretical explorations, not evidence of operational technologies
- The research was conducted by qualified physicists (including Puthoff and Davis) but addressed topics far beyond current engineering capabilities
- No evidence of reverse-engineered alien technology is contained in the released DIRDs

### 8.3 AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 (March 2024)

**What it shows:**
- AARO reviewed all official USG investigatory efforts since 1945
- Conducted ~30 interviews with relevant personnel
- Found "no empirical evidence" for claims of reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology
- Determined claims involving specific people, locations, and documents "are inaccurate"
- Disproved the "majority" of interviewees' claims (some still under evaluation)
- Company executives named by interviewees denied on record possessing off-world technology
- No USG investigation, academic research, or review panel has confirmed any UAP represents extraterrestrial technology

**Criticism of AARO report:**
- Multiple whistleblowers and UAP advocates have challenged AARO's findings
- Some members of Congress expressed dissatisfaction with AARO's investigation
- The report's critics argue it was not given proper access to all relevant SAPs
- The report's defenders note it is the most comprehensive official review conducted

### 8.4 Military Bolide Data Release (2022)

**What it shows:**
- US military sensors (designed for missile detection) incidentally collected data on ~1,000 bolide events since 1988
- This data was classified to protect sensor capabilities, not to hide threats
- Declassification was specifically for planetary defense purposes
- Demonstrates that classification of space-related data occurs for reasons of sensor security, not threat suppression

### 8.5 Congressional UAP Records

**Key documented actions:**
- May 2022: First public congressional UAP hearing in 50+ years
- July 2023: House Oversight Committee hearing with Grusch testimony
- November 2024: Additional hearing led by Rep. Nancy Mace
- December 2023: Partial UAP Disclosure Act enacted into law
  - Established UAP Records Collection at National Archives
  - Required federal agencies to review and identify UAP records
  - Framework for disclosure with national security postponement provisions
  - Original version significantly watered down during negotiations

## 10. ASSESSMENT: Historical Context for Evaluating the Catastrophe Hypothesis

### 10.1 What the Historical Record Establishes

1. **The US government has funded decades of unconventional research.** From SRI remote viewing (1972-1995) to AAWSAP/AATIP (2007-2012+), there is a documented pattern of government agencies funding research into phenomena that mainstream science considers speculative or fringe. This is verified by FOIA documents and congressional records.

2. **A small, interconnected network has driven much of this research.** The Puthoff-Davis-Green-Vallee-Bigelow network has been involved in virtually every major government-adjacent anomalous phenomena program for 50+ years. Their involvement is documented, but this concentration raises questions about whether programs represent independent assessments or a self-reinforcing community.

3. **Classification of space-related data does occur, but for sensor-security reasons.** The 2022 declassification of bolide data demonstrates that military classification of astronomical data is real but is driven by protecting sensor capabilities, not suppressing threat information. NASA's PDCO operates transparently.

4. **Planetary defense is an open, civilian-led effort.** NASA's PDCO, CNEOS, DART mission, and FEMA tabletop exercises are publicly documented and internationally collaborative. There is no documented evidence of a parallel classified planetary defense apparatus.

5. **The AARO report found no evidence of crash retrieval or reverse engineering programs.** The most comprehensive official review (2024) explicitly contradicted claims of hidden programs. Whether one accepts AARO's findings depends on whether one believes AARO had full access to all relevant information.

6. **Congressional interest is genuine but has produced no confirmed extraordinary evidence.** Despite multiple hearings, whistleblower protections, and partial disclosure legislation, no publicly available evidence of non-human technology, hidden threat knowledge, or catastrophe-related programs has emerged from the congressional process.

### 10.2 What the Historical Record Does NOT Establish

1. **No documented connection between UAP programs and asteroid/planetary defense.** These are separate streams: planetary defense is run by NASA's PDCO with full transparency; UAP programs have been military/intelligence programs focused on aerospace threats (whether foreign or unknown). No declassified document connects them.

2. **No evidence of classified knowledge of an impending catastrophe.** While the government classifies certain space-related data (sensor capabilities), there is no documented case of the government hiding knowledge of a genuine impact threat. The PDCO's mandate is explicitly to provide early warning.

3. **No verified government confirmation of non-human intelligence or technology.** Every official government investigation from Blue Book (1952-1969) through AARO (2022-present) has concluded that no evidence confirms extraterrestrial technology. Individual whistleblowers claim otherwise, but their claims remain unverified.

4. **Remote viewing programs produced no confirmed intelligence about astronomical threats.** The Stargate program is well-documented, but there is no declassified evidence of remote viewers identifying asteroid threats, alien civilizations, or impending catastrophes.

### 10.3 Evaluative Framework

For any claim connecting these programs to a catastrophe hypothesis, the following questions should be applied:

- **Is the source a FOIA-released document, congressional record, or official government report?** If not, it is unverified.
- **Is the claim based on firsthand knowledge or secondhand reporting?** Grusch's claims, for example, are explicitly secondhand.
- **Does the claim rely on the small Puthoff-Davis-Green-Bigelow network?** If so, consider whether it represents independent corroboration or network reinforcement.
- **Does the AARO Historical Record Report address the claim?** If so, what was AARO's finding?
- **Is there a simpler explanation?** Classification of sensor data, bureaucratic secrecy, and institutional inertia can explain much of the documented record without invoking extraordinary hypotheses.

## 11. UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS

1. **What is in the 38th DIRD?** The DIA released 37 of 38 DIRDs under FOIA. The topic and reason for withholding the 38th document remain unknown.

2. **What was the full scope of AATIP?** The Pentagon's contradictory statements about Elizondo's role have never been reconciled. Was AATIP a continuation of AAWSAP, a separate program, or an informal effort?

3. **What did the IC Inspector General find regarding Grusch's complaint?** The IG determined it was "credible and urgent" for investigation purposes, but the results of any investigation remain classified.

4. **Did AARO have full access to all relevant SAPs?** Critics argue AARO was denied access to certain Special Access Programs. If true, AARO's "no evidence" finding would be limited by its access.

5. **What accounts for the Pentagon's contradictory statements about AATIP/Elizondo?** This remains unexplained and could reflect bureaucratic confusion, deliberate obfuscation, or something else.

6. **Why did three senior senators fund AAWSAP through the black budget?** Reid, Stevens, and Inouye were serious legislators. Their decision to fund this program through classified channels and award it to Bigelow raises questions about what motivated them that remain partially answered.

7. **What role, if any, do defense contractors play in undisclosed programs?** The Wilson-Davis memo (if authentic) and Grusch's testimony both point to private contractors. AARO interviewed contractor executives who denied the claims. The matter is unresolved.

8. **Is there classified planetary defense information beyond sensor capabilities?** The 2022 bolide data release shows classification exists for sensor security. Whether any planetary threat assessments have been classified is unknown.

9. **What happened to AAWSAP's more unconventional research findings?** The program investigated Skinwalker Ranch, consciousness effects, and anomalous phenomena. The disposition of this research and whether it continued after 2012 is poorly documented.

10. **Why has the UAP Disclosure Act been repeatedly weakened?** The original legislation was significantly diluted during congressional negotiations. Whether this reflects normal legislative compromise, active opposition from stakeholders, or national security concerns is debated.

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This report is based on 15+ web searches conducted on 2026-08-10, cross-referencing government sources (FOIA archives, congressional records, official DoD/NASA publications), major investigative journalism (NYT, The Intercept, Vice), and secondary sources. Every effort has been made to distinguish documented facts from allegations, and to assign appropriate source tiers. Where claims conflict, both positions are presented.

The purpose of this report is CONTEXT for evaluating claims -- not confirmation or denial of any particular hypothesis. The documented record shows real programs, real funding, and real people, but the extraordinary claims built atop that foundation remain unverified by any official investigation.
