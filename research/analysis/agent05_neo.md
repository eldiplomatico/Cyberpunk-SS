# AGENT 05 -- NEAR-EARTH OBJECT / COMET / ASTEROID RESEARCH DATASET

## Investigation Context

This report compiles an astronomical dataset of near-Earth objects (NEOs), potentially hazardous asteroids (PHAs), and close approaches relevant to evaluating claims that certain individuals (Hal Puthoff, Dan Sherman, and others) allegedly warned of a coming catastrophic event possibly involving an asteroid or comet impact. The dataset is constructed from authoritative astronomical sources (NASA/JPL, ESA, Minor Planet Center, peer-reviewed literature) to provide the empirical baseline against which any such claims must be measured.

### PHA Definition

A Potentially Hazardous Asteroid is defined by two criteria:
- Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance (MOID) with Earth of <= 0.05 AU (~7.5 million km)
- Absolute magnitude (H) of 22.0 or brighter (implying diameter >= ~100-140 m)

### Current Survey Capabilities

- **Ground-based surveys**: Catalina Sky Survey (CSS), Pan-STARRS, ATLAS -- responsible for bulk of discoveries
- **Space-based**: NEOWISE (decommissioned 2024), NEO Surveyor (launch NET September 2027)
- **Planned/operational**: Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST) -- expected to discover ~127,000 NEOs over 10-year survey
- **NEO Surveyor**: Will find >= 2/3 of NEAs > 140 m within 5-year baseline survey using infrared detection
- **Current completeness**: ~38% for objects > 140 m; ~90%+ for objects > 1 km

### Hazard Assessment Systems

**Torino Scale** (public communication, 0-10 integer scale):
- 0 = No hazard; likelihood of collision is zero or so low as to be effectively zero
- 1 = Normal; merits careful monitoring
- 2-4 = Meriting attention; close encounter with 1%+ chance of collision capable of localized destruction
- 5-7 = Threatening; close encounter with significant threat of collision
- 8-10 = Certain collisions at various scales
- **Current status (April 2026): ALL cataloged asteroids rate 0 on the Torino Scale for the next 100 years**

**Palermo Scale** (technical, logarithmic, continuous):
- Negative values = event less likely than background
- 0 = as likely as background random impact
- Positive values = event exceeds background probability
- **Current status (April 2026): No asteroid has cumulative rating above 0. Only two objects (1950 DA and Bennu) rate between -2 and 0**

## 6. BASE-RATE ANALYSIS: HOW COMMON ARE CLOSE APPROACHES?

### Frequency of Close Approaches

| Threshold | Approx. Annual Frequency | Context |
|-----------|-------------------------|---------|
| Within 1 LD (384,400 km) | ~150-200+ per year | 2025 had 189 known approaches < 1 LD |
| Within 0.5 LD (~192,000 km) | ~30-50 per year | Common but typically small objects |
| Within geostationary orbit (~36,000 km) | ~5-10 per year | Almost always very small (< 10 m) |
| Actual atmosphere entry (detected) | ~10-15 per year (for bolides) | Small objects, typically 1-5 m |
| Within 1 LD AND diameter > 100 m | ~1-3 per decade | Rare but not unprecedented |
| Within 1 LD AND diameter > 1 km | ~1-2 per century | Extremely rare for known objects |

### Background Impact Rate

| Size Class | Mean Interval Between Impacts | Energy Equivalent |
|------------|------------------------------|-------------------|
| ~4 m (Chelyabinsk-class bolide) | ~1 per year | ~500 kilotons TNT |
| ~20 m (Chelyabinsk airburst) | ~50-100 years | ~500 kilotons TNT |
| ~50 m (Tunguska-class) | ~500-1,000 years | ~10-15 megatons TNT |
| ~140 m (city-destroyer) | ~10,000-20,000 years | ~300 megatons TNT |
| ~1 km (regional catastrophe) | ~500,000 years | ~100,000 megatons TNT |
| ~10 km (global extinction) | ~100 million years | ~100 million megatons TNT |

### Key Implication for Evaluating Claims

The base rate of asteroid close approaches is HIGH. With ~150-200+ objects passing within lunar distance annually, the probability of "something passing close" in any given year is essentially 100%. However, the probability of a LARGE, DANGEROUS object impacting Earth in any given decade is vanishingly small:

- Probability of a > 140 m impactor in any given year: ~0.005-0.01% (1/10,000 to 1/20,000)
- Probability of a > 1 km impactor in any given year: ~0.0002% (1/500,000)
- Probability of a > 10 km impactor in any given year: ~0.000001% (1/100,000,000)

Any prediction of "an asteroid will pass close to Earth" in a given period is trivially easy to fulfill because it happens constantly. The evidentiary bar for a meaningful prediction must include SPECIFIC identification: object name, size, date, distance, and ideally orbital parameters.

## 9. ASSESSMENT OF ASTRONOMICAL EVIDENCE

### Summary Findings

1. **No known asteroid or comet poses a credible impact threat to Earth in the 2025-2040 window.** All cataloged objects currently rate Torino Scale 0 for the next 100 years.

2. **The most dramatic upcoming event is Apophis's 2029 flyby**, which will bring a 375-m asteroid within 31,600 km of Earth's surface -- closer than geostationary satellites. However, this has been confirmed to pose ZERO impact risk.

### Analytical Assessment

The astronomical evidence provides NO support for any alleged catastrophe prediction involving an asteroid or comet impact in the 2025-2040 timeframe. The data shows:

- **Completeness**: We have cataloged >90% of NEAs > 1 km and ~38% of NEAs > 140 m. While undiscovered objects exist, the probability of a large undiscovered impactor on a collision course with Earth in the near term is extremely low (estimated at <0.01% per decade for objects > 140 m).

- **Monitoring**: NASA Sentry-II and ESA Meerkat continuously scan the catalog for potential impacts. No object currently rates above Torino 0.

- **Planetary defense capability**: DART successfully demonstrated kinetic impactor deflection technology (33-minute orbital period change), providing a viable defense against detected threats.

- **Upcoming capability**: NEO Surveyor (launch 2027) and Vera Rubin Observatory will dramatically increase detection capabilities for objects > 140 m, further reducing the probability of an undetected threatening object.

## 10. UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS

1. **Undiscovered objects**: Only ~38% of NEAs > 140 m have been cataloged. An undiscovered object could theoretically appear with short warning time. However, this is a generic risk that applies always, not a specific threat connected to any particular prediction.

2. **Long-period comets**: Comets arriving from the Oort cloud (like C/2023 A3 Tsuchinshan-ATLAS) provide much less warning time than asteroids in well-tracked orbits. A large long-period comet could conceivably appear with only months to years of warning. This remains the most significant gap in planetary defense -- but no specific threatening comet has been identified.

3. **Bennu 2135 encounter**: The 2135 close approach will be the critical event that determines whether Bennu's orbit shifts toward or away from the 2182 impact keyhole. This is the most important planetary defense monitoring event of the coming century, but it is 109 years away.
