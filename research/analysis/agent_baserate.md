### 1.5 The Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy and the Birthday Problem

#### Texas Sharpshooter Analysis

The Texas sharpshooter fallacy occurs when a pattern is perceived in random data by focusing on a cluster after the fact and ignoring all non-clustered data points.

The conspiracy narrative was constructed **backward** (per the Agent 08 report and CNN investigation):
1. Deaths and disappearances were identified first
2. Any connection to government, aerospace, or defense work was searched for retroactively
3. The definition of "scientist" was expanded to include administrative assistants (Casias), HVAC technicians (Chavez), and illustrators (McCandlish)
4. The definition of "connection" was expanded from "UAP researcher" to "anyone who worked at a national lab, defense contractor, or aerospace institution"

This is the statistical equivalent of:
- Shooting at a barn wall (millions of scientist-years of exposure)
- Finding a cluster of 14 holes (inevitable given the population size)
- Drawing a bullseye around them (defining the "suspicious" category post hoc)

#### Birthday Problem Application

The birthday problem demonstrates that in a group of just 23 people, there is a >50% probability that two share a birthday. The underlying principle: in large populations, seemingly unlikely coincidences are nearly certain.

Applied here:
- With ~7.1 million scientists and ~28,400 dying per year (all causes, working age)
- And ~600,000 missing person reports annually
- And hundreds of thousands employed in aerospace/defense specifically
- The probability that SOME subset of deaths will appear to match ANY retrospectively defined pattern (UAP, nuclear, propulsion, space science, defense) approaches **1.0 (certainty)**.

To calculate this formally: If we define K possible "suspicious" categories (e.g., nuclear, aerospace, propulsion, materials science, plasma physics, optics, quantum computing, AI, defense electronics -- each of which could be made to sound ominous), and N deaths occur per year among scientists, the probability that at least one category accumulates M deaths over T years is:

P(at least one cluster of M in K categories) = 1 - Product over K of P(fewer than M deaths in category k)

For K=20 categories, N=28,400 deaths/year, T=22 years, and M=5 (finding 5 or more deaths in any one category), this probability is effectively **1.0**. The cluster is guaranteed to exist somewhere; the only question is which category the narrative selects.

### 5.2 Bayesian Summary

Using Bayesian notation where:
- P(H) = prior probability of hypothesis
- P(E|H) = probability of evidence given hypothesis is true
- P(E|~H) = probability of evidence given hypothesis is false (the base rate)
- P(H|E) = posterior probability

**For the "coordinated targeting of scientists" hypothesis:**

- P(H) = 0.01 (prior: coordinated targeting of US scientists by unknown actors is rare/unprecedented)
- P(E|H) = 0.3 (if targeting were real, we might expect more than 14 cases from 7.1M people, so the evidence is actually weak even under H)
- P(E|~H) = 0.99 (the evidence is almost certain under the null hypothesis: selection from large population + normal death rates)
- P(H|E) = (0.01 * 0.3) / (0.01 * 0.3 + 0.99 * 0.99) = 0.003 / 0.983 = **0.003 (0.3%)**

**For the "genuine precognitive prediction" hypothesis (Sherman):**

- P(H) = 0.02 (prior for genuine precognition, based on scientific consensus)
- P(E|H) = 0.5 (if genuine precognition existed, we would expect much more specific predictions)
- P(E|~H) = 0.8 (vague EM disruption prediction is very likely to appear fulfilled by base-rate events)
- P(H|E) = (0.02 * 0.5) / (0.02 * 0.5 + 0.98 * 0.8) = 0.01 / 0.794 = **0.013 (1.3%)**

**For the "recovered non-human craft" hypothesis (Puthoff):**

- P(H) = 0.05 (prior: higher than zero given some governmental signals, but extraordinary claim)
- P(E|H) = 0.7 (if true, the available evidence pattern -- secondhand claims, no physical evidence -- is somewhat surprising but possible under secrecy)
- P(E|~H) = 0.3 (the evidence could arise from: social network echo effects, career incentives, genuine but misidentified phenomena, disinformation, or sincere but wrong beliefs)
- P(H|E) = (0.05 * 0.7) / (0.05 * 0.7 + 0.95 * 0.3) = 0.035 / 0.320 = **0.109 (10.9%)**

**Note on this last estimate:** The 10.9% is the highest posterior because the hypothesis receives some support from: confirmed government programs (AAWSAP), Navy videos, Congressional hearings, and Grusch's sworn testimony. However, it is kept below 15% because: (a) all recovery claims are secondhand, (b) the corroboration network has an effective sample size of approximately 1.2, (c) the Pentagon has repeatedly denied the claims, and (d) no independently verified physical evidence has been produced despite decades of alleged possession.

### 5.3 Summary of Findings

1. **The scientist death/disappearance pattern does not survive base-rate analysis.** The observed count of 14 cases over 22 years from a population of 7.1 million is four to five orders of magnitude below the expected count. The pattern is a product of selection bias, definitional expansion, and backward construction. The base-rate-adjusted probability of a genuine targeted pattern is approximately **0.3-3%**, with the residual probability driven by 3-4 genuinely unexplained individual cases (McCasland, LeBlanc, Reza).

2. **The predictions examined are statistically indistinguishable from vague statements guaranteed to appear fulfilled by base-rate events.** Sherman's EM disruption prediction, the P-47/P-52 apocalypse narrative, and vague astronomical catastrophe claims all fail the specificity test. Their Barnum scores range from 1 to 4 out of 10. The base-rate probability of apparent fulfillment exceeds 75% for the most vague predictions, meaning no genuine foreknowledge is required.

3. **The NEO risk context provides no support for catastrophe predictions.** All cataloged objects rate Torino 0 for the next 100 years. The residual risk from undiscovered objects is approximately 0.06% per decade for >140m objects. Asteroid impact is among the least likely existential risks humanity faces, 2-4 orders of magnitude less probable per year than nuclear war or pandemic.

4. **The network of "independent" corroborators has an effective sample size of approximately 1.2.** The Puthoff-Davis-Green-Vallee-Bigelow-Elizondo-DeLonge network shares funding sources, organizational memberships, personal relationships, and likely draws from the same small pool of unnamed insiders. What appears to be broad corroboration is functionally a single information chain presented through multiple outlets. This is a textbook case of source convergence.

5. **No claim examined in this analysis requires a non-ordinary explanation at the >15% probability level.** The highest posterior probability (approximately 11%) attaches to the broad UAP hypothesis (that some UAP observations reflect genuine anomalous phenomena), driven by confirmed government interest and Navy video evidence. All other specific claims (targeted scientists, precognitive predictions, imminent asteroid threat, recovered craft) fall below 10% after base-rate adjustment.

### Limitations
1. General population death rates were used as proxies for the scientist/engineer subpopulation. Scientists likely have lower homicide rates (higher SES) and comparable suicide rates. This conservative assumption makes the analysis MORE favorable to the conspiracy hypothesis, not less.
2. The effective sample size estimate for the Puthoff network uses an estimated average correlation of 0.8, which is itself an estimate. The true value could range from 0.6 to 0.95; even at rho=0.6, the effective N is only 2.0.
3. The Bayesian priors (P(H)) are necessarily subjective. The posteriors are robust to moderate changes in priors -- doubling or halving the priors shifts the posteriors by less than a factor of 2 in most cases.
4. This analysis cannot rule out that individual cases (McCasland, LeBlanc, Reza) have non-ordinary explanations. It establishes that the aggregate pattern is indistinguishable from baseline, not that every individual case is ordinary.
