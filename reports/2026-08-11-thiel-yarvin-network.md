# Thiel-Yarvin Network: ACH Investigation

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Method:** Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (Heuer)
**Question:** What explains the observed pattern of Peter Thiel funding Curtis Yarvin's intellectual enterprise, placing network members in senior government positions, and securing tens of billions in government contracts for portfolio companies — ideology-driven coordination, economically-motivated regulatory capture, parallel independent activities, or standard mega-donor behavior?
**Prior report:** reports/2026-08-11-thiel-yarvin-network-analysis.md

## BLUF

**Matrix Changes This Cycle**

1. **H1 (Ideology-First Pipeline) holds at 0 inconsistencies.** New evidence — including DOGE advisers confirming Yarvin's intellectual influence, the Seasteading Institute's $1.7M in financially irrational ideological spending, and Yarvin's own criticism that DOGE is too incremental — all score Consistent with ideology as the primary driver. No evidence contradicts this hypothesis.
2. **H2 (Economics-First) enters at 2 inconsistencies, breaking the prior cycle's tie with H1.** Two pieces of discriminating evidence now score Inconsistent: (a) Thiel's $1.7M Seasteading investment had no financial return pathway, which contradicts economics-first motivation; (b) Yarvin's public dissatisfaction with DOGE's execution contradicts the expectation that an economics-first actor would be satisfied with DOGE's deregulatory output. The prior report's H2 (Market Friction/Opportunism) carried 0 inconsistencies because the informal scoring did not test these items.
3. **H3 (Genuine Parallel Pursuit) enters at 5 inconsistencies.** Five evidence items show deliberate coordination between ideological and financial activities, contradicting the claim that these are independent tracks.
4. **H4 (Standard Donor Dynamics / Null Case) enters at 7 inconsistencies.** The Thiel network is structurally different from Koch, Soros, or Bloomberg donor operations across seven dimensions, eliminating the null case that this is normal mega-donor behavior.

**H1 (Ideology-First Pipeline) leads with 0 inconsistencies, followed by H2 at 2.** We assess with moderate confidence that anti-democratic ideology is the primary driver of the Thiel-Yarvin network, with financial returns as a reinforcing byproduct rather than the motivating force. The key discriminator is Thiel's demonstrated willingness to spend capital on ideological projects with no financial return pathway (Seasteading), combined with Yarvin's genuine dissatisfaction that DOGE's disruption is not radical enough — behavior inconsistent with an economics-first actor who should be satisfied with $45B in government contract ceilings.

## Findings

### Finding Group 1: Ideological Foundation and Temporal Sequence

**F1. Thiel published an explicit rejection of democracy before making political investments.**

Thiel's 2009 Cato Institute essay "The Education of a Libertarian" states: "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible." This ideological position preceded his political investments (Vance funding began 2021, Founders Fund/Tlon investment 2013). The temporal sequence — ideology articulated, then capital deployed — is consistent with ideology driving investment, not the reverse.

*Source tier: Institutional (Cato Unbound, primary source). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H4 (I). Diagnostic.*

**F2. Yarvin's 2012 RAGE concept was operationalized as DOGE in 2025.**

Yarvin's 2012 speech "How to Reboot the US Government" proposed RAGE (Retire All Government Employees): mass dismissal of federal workers as precondition for authoritarian restructuring. In 2025, DOGE implemented a recognizable variant. Two DOGE advisers told the Washington Post that Yarvin offered "the most crisp articulation" of what DOGE is trying to achieve, and that "it's an open secret that everyone in policymaking roles has read Yarvin."

*Source tier: News (Washington Post, two advisers on record). Confidence: Moderate (anonymous but specific). Affects: H1 (C), H3 (I), H4 (I). Diagnostic.*

**F3. Yarvin publicly criticized DOGE as too incremental.**

In May 2025, Yarvin told the Washington Post he "scorns" DOGE's execution, comparing it to "an orchestra of chimpanzees trying to perform Wagner." His criticism is that DOGE is insufficiently radical — the "reboot" should be total, not piecemeal. This is inconsistent with an economics-first model (H2), where Yarvin should be satisfied that DOGE is delivering deregulation and contract opportunities. His dissatisfaction with the *degree* of disruption indicates genuine ideological commitment.

*Source tier: News (Washington Post, direct quotes). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H2 (I). Diagnostic.*

### Finding Group 2: Financial Circuit and Return on Political Investment

**F4. Thiel-linked companies hold ~$45B in government contract ceilings (2025-2026).**

Palantir holds $13.7B in multi-year contract ceilings, including a 10-year, $10B Army enterprise agreement consolidating 75 contracts. Anduril holds a 10-year, $20B Army enterprise contract (Project Linchpin) plus a $2B border surveillance IDIQ with ~$818M obligated. Combined potential value exceeds $45B. These ceilings represent maximum potential value, not obligated amounts, but the scale is extraordinary for companies this young.

*Source tier: Institutional (government contract records, Fed-Spend analysis). Confidence: High. Affects: H2 (C), H3 (C). Non-diagnostic (C or A across all hypotheses).*

**F5. Palantir's lobbying spending rose from $2.4M (2020) to $6.1M (2025).**

A 154% increase in lobbying expenditure during the period when Thiel network members were entering government. Consistent with all hypotheses — defense companies increase lobbying — but the timing correlation with political placements is notable.

*Source tier: Institutional (lobbying disclosures). Confidence: High. Affects: H2 (C). Non-diagnostic.*

**F6. David Sacks retained 449 AI investments while serving as White House AI/Crypto Czar.**

The New York Times revealed Sacks holds 20 crypto investments and 449 AI-related ones. Despite divesting over $200 million in assets, he retained stakes in companies affected by policies he shapes, including connections between BitGo and the GENIUS Act stablecoin legislation. The Office of Government Ethics approved his public waivers, but Senator Warren launched a congressional investigation.

*Source tier: News (New York Times investigation) + Institutional (OGE filings, Warren investigation). Confidence: High. Affects: H2 (C). Non-diagnostic.*

**F7. Palantir advises the State Department on surveillance and free speech (August 2026).**

The Intercept reported that Palantir — a company built on digital surveillance — is advising the State Department on "countering digital surveillance." A surveillance company advising on surveillance regulation represents a degree of regulatory integration that is inconsistent with the parallel-tracks model (H3) and standard donor behavior (H4).

*Source tier: News (The Intercept, investigative). Confidence: Moderate (single outlet). Affects: H1 (C), H3 (I), H4 (I). Diagnostic.*

### Finding Group 3: Network Structure and Placement Pipeline

**F8. Thiel personally introduced Vance to Trump at Mar-a-Lago (2021).**

This introduction was not incidental — Vance had been publicly critical of Trump, and the introduction was a deliberate act to place Thiel's political protégé within Trump's orbit. Combined with the subsequent $15M donation (largest single Senate donation in U.S. history), this constitutes an active pipeline from patron to politician to power.

*Source tier: News (multiple outlets). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H2 (C), H3 (I). Diagnostic (contradicts independent-tracks model).*

**F9. Thiel served on the transition team vetting government appointments.**

Thiel was among approximately 100 aides, policy experts, and officials vetting, interviewing, and recommending individuals for top cabinet and staff roles. This is direct coordination between the person funding ideology (Yarvin), the person funding politics (Vance), and the person whose companies benefit from government contracts (Palantir, Anduril) — the same person in all three roles.

*Source tier: News (multiple outlets). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H2 (C), H3 (I). Diagnostic.*

**F10. Network placements span multiple domains: VP (Vance), AI/Crypto policy (Sacks), HHS (O'Neill), defense/surveillance (Palantir, Anduril).**

Jim O'Neill, former CEO of Thiel's personal foundation, was appointed Deputy Secretary of HHS. David Sacks, PayPal Mafia colleague, named White House AI/Crypto Czar. These are not political donors receiving ambassadorships — they are Thiel's direct employees and business partners placed in operationally significant roles.

*Source tier: News (confirmed appointments). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H2 (C). Non-diagnostic.*

**F11. Thiel's $15M Vance donation was the largest single Senate donation in U.S. history.**

Combined with $10M+ to Blake Masters in Arizona (who lost). The scale exceeds normal donor behavior, though other mega-donors have spent comparable totals across multiple candidates. The distinction is concentration: two candidates, both Thiel protégés, both carriers of Yarvin-influenced ideas.

*Source tier: Institutional (FEC records). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H4 (I). Diagnostic.*

### Finding Group 4: Discriminating Evidence — Ideology vs. Economics

**F12. Thiel invested $1.7M in the Seasteading Institute, a project with no financial return pathway.**

The Seasteading Institute, co-founded with Patri Friedman in 2008, aimed to build floating "startup governments" — libertarian societies free from democratic regulation. Thiel contributed approximately $1.7M including matching grants. The project failed when French Polynesia backed away from an agreement. Thiel resigned from the board in 2011. This investment had no plausible financial return — it was a pure ideological expenditure. This is inconsistent with H2 (economics-first): a rationally self-interested actor would not spend $1.7M on a project that cannot generate returns. It is consistent with H1: an ideology-first actor invests in ideological projects even when they lose money.

*Source tier: News (multiple outlets, Phys.org, Yahoo News). Confidence: High. Affects: H1 (C), H2 (I), H4 (I). **Key discriminator between H1 and H2.***

**F13. Thiel's donor model is structurally different from Koch, Soros, and Bloomberg operations.**

Koch donors operate through institutional channels: university grants, think-tank funding, issue-advocacy PACs, and broad-based donor consortia. Soros and Bloomberg operate through established philanthropic foundations and direct candidate PAC donations. Thiel operates through VC investments (Founders Fund), personal relationships (introducing Vance to Trump), and direct employee placement (O'Neill from his personal foundation). The mechanism is personal and relational, not institutional — a patron-philosopher model, not a think-tank model.

*Source tier: Analysis/News (academic studies, journalistic comparisons). Confidence: Moderate. Affects: H1 (C), H4 (I). Diagnostic.*

### Finding Group 5: Counterevidence and Noise

**F14. Blake Masters lost the Arizona Senate race (2022).**

Thiel's $10M+ investment in Masters produced no political return. This is consistent with H2 (normal investment risk), H3 (imperfect correlation), and H4 (donors back losers), but ambiguous for H1 — electoral losses don't disprove ideology. The 50% success rate on Thiel's two major Senate investments is notable but not dispositive.

*Source tier: Institutional (election results). Confidence: High. Non-diagnostic.*

**F15. Network factionalism: Yarvin vs. Musk, Thiel's retreat from public donations.**

Yarvin has never met Musk. Yarvin criticizes DOGE's execution. Thiel has stepped back from public political donations. These fractures indicate the network is not a monolithic conspiracy but an alignment of actors with overlapping-but-distinct goals. Consistent with H2 (rational actors diverge when interests diverge), H3 (parallel tracks produce factionalism), and H4 (donor networks have internal dynamics). Ambiguous for H1 — ideological movements routinely have factional disputes.

*Source tier: News (Washington Post, multiple outlets). Confidence: Moderate. Non-diagnostic.*

**F16. Yarvin denies direct advisory role; says he is "not involved" with DOGE.**

Yarvin told the Washington Post he was "not involved" with DOGE and has "never met" Musk. However, two DOGE advisers contradicted this by citing Yarvin as a direct intellectual influence. The denial itself is ambiguous — it could reflect genuine distance or strategic deniability. The advisers' on-record citations carry more evidentiary weight than the subject's self-denial.

*Source tier: News (Washington Post). Confidence: Moderate. Affects: H1 (A), H3 (A). Non-diagnostic (competing claims cancel).*

## Hypotheses

### H1: Ideology-First Pipeline
Anti-democratic ideology is the primary driver of the Thiel-Yarvin network. Thiel deploys capital in service of an intellectual framework (Yarvin's neoreactionary theory) that calls for replacing democratic governance with corporate-monarchical structure. Financial returns from Palantir, Anduril, and other portfolio companies are a reinforcing byproduct, not the motivating force.

**Falsifier:** Evidence that Thiel consistently prioritizes financial returns over ideological goals — e.g., abandoning an ideological position when it threatens portfolio value, or choosing a financially optimal but ideologically neutral political investment over a financially suboptimal but ideologically aligned one.

### H2: Economics-First with Ideological Cover
Financial returns are the primary driver. Yarvin's intellectual framework provides legitimacy and coherence to what is fundamentally regulatory capture — placing allies in government to shape procurement, regulation, and policy in ways that benefit Thiel's portfolio. Ideology is branding, not motivation.

**Falsifier:** Evidence that Thiel makes financially costly ideological investments with no return pathway — investments that an economically rational actor would not make. (Seasteading satisfies this falsifier.)

### H3: Genuine Parallel Pursuit
Ideology and economics are independent parallel tracks that happen to co-occur in the same person. Thiel has genuine ideological beliefs AND separate financial interests; the two are not causally connected. Yarvin's influence on policy is organic (ideas spread independently), not a pipeline Thiel constructed.

**Falsifier:** Evidence of deliberate coordination between ideological activities and financial positioning — actions that bridge the tracks rather than running them independently. (Thiel introducing Vance to Trump, Thiel vetting appointments, DOGE advisers citing Yarvin all satisfy this falsifier.)

### H4: Standard Donor Dynamics (Null Case)
The Thiel-Yarvin pattern is normal mega-donor behavior — no different in kind from Koch, Soros, or Bloomberg operations. Wealthy donors fund ideas, fund candidates, place allies, and benefit from friendly policy environments. The Thiel network's scale and style are within normal parameters for American political influence.

**Falsifier:** Evidence that the Thiel model is structurally different from other mega-donor operations — different mechanisms, different ideological content, different placement patterns. (Multiple evidence items satisfy this falsifier.)

## Deception & Denial Analysis

### Load-Bearing Evidence Item 1: DOGE Advisers Citing Yarvin (E8/E15)

**Current effect:** Scores C for H1 and I for H3/H4. This is the primary evidence that ideological transmission is real — that Yarvin's framework is not just ambient intellectual content but an active operating system for policymakers.

**Reversal scenario:** The DOGE advisers' claims are performative — they cite Yarvin for credibility within the network or to signal alignment, but Yarvin's ideas did not actually drive policy decisions. DOGE's actions would have been the same without Yarvin's intellectual architecture, following standard deregulation playbooks.

**Matrix impact:** If reversed, H3 loses two inconsistencies (E8, E15), reducing from 5 to 3. H1 loses its strongest evidence of ideological transmission. H3 and H1 converge — the evidence can no longer distinguish pipeline from parallel tracks.

**Assessment:** This is the investigation's single most important evidence item. It rests on anonymous sourcing (two DOGE advisers, people "familiar with DOGE leadership's thinking") via the Washington Post. The sourcing is moderate-confidence — WaPo's editorial standards provide a baseline, but the claims are unverifiable outside the sourcing relationship.

### Load-Bearing Evidence Item 2: Seasteading Institute Investment (E14)

**Current effect:** Scores C for H1 and I for H2/H4. This is the key discriminator between ideology-first and economics-first — a financially irrational ideological investment.

**Reversal scenario:** Seasteading was actually a speculative business investment — an attempt to create sovereign real estate or regulatory arbitrage zones. Thiel expected financial returns from selling citizenship or residency in floating nations, and the failure was an investment loss, not an ideological expenditure.

**Matrix impact:** If reversed, H2 loses one inconsistency (E14), narrowing from 2 to 1. The H1-vs-H2 discrimination weakens significantly.

**Assessment:** The Seasteading Institute's own mission statement and public communications describe the project in ideological terms (libertarian governance experiments), not financial ones. No reporting suggests a financial return pathway existed. However, the reversal scenario is plausible if we consider Thiel's broader interest in regulatory arbitrage (PayPal, Palantir's data sovereignty work). Confidence in the current scoring is moderate.

### Load-Bearing Evidence Item 3: Yarvin Scorning DOGE (E9)

**Current effect:** Scores C for H1 and I for H2. If economics were primary, Yarvin should be satisfied with DOGE's output — it's delivering deregulation and contract opportunities.

**Reversal scenario:** Yarvin's criticism is strategic brand positioning — he maintains his radical credentials by criticizing implementation as insufficiently radical, regardless of whether he's genuinely dissatisfied. The criticism is marketing, not ideology.

**Matrix impact:** If reversed, H2 loses one inconsistency (E9), narrowing from 2 to 1.

**Assessment:** Yarvin's criticism is specific and substantive — he wants a total "reboot," not piecemeal cuts. The level of detail in his criticism (comparing DOGE to "chimpanzees performing Wagner") suggests genuine intellectual engagement, not mere positioning. However, Yarvin is a professional provocateur, and strategic criticism is within his behavioral range.

### Load-Bearing Evidence Item 4: Thiel Introducing Vance to Trump (E4)

**Current effect:** Scores C for H1/H2 and I for H3. Active coordination between political and financial tracks.

**Reversal scenario:** The introduction was social — Thiel introduces many people to Trump, and Vance's subsequent rise was driven by Vance's own political talent, not Thiel's pipeline engineering.

**Matrix impact:** If reversed, H3 loses one inconsistency (E4), from 5 to 4. H3 remains heavily disconfirmed.

**Assessment:** The introduction was clearly strategic — Vance had been publicly anti-Trump ("cultural heroin"), and the introduction was a deliberate act of political alignment. The $15M donation followed the introduction. Reversal is implausible.

## Cross-Domain Bridge Analysis

### 6a. Bridge Mapping

**Bridge 1: Financial → Political**
- **Domains connected:** Financial capital → Political power
- **Mechanism:** Thiel capital ($15M Vance, $10M+ Masters, transition team participation) → Senate seat → Vice Presidency → administration populated by network members
- **Evidence status:** Confirmed. FEC records document the donations. Multiple sources confirm the Vance-Trump introduction and transition team role.
- **Strength:** Central to the assessment. If this bridge is severed (if Thiel's donations are shown to have no causal relationship to Vance's rise), the pipeline model collapses.

**Bridge 2: Ideological → Political**
- **Domains connected:** Intellectual/ideological framework → Policy implementation
- **Mechanism:** Yarvin's writings (NRx theory, RAGE, Gray Mirror) → DOGE policy, administrative state dismantlement, "reboot" concept
- **Evidence status:** Confirmed. DOGE advisers cite Yarvin directly. "Open secret" that policymakers have read Yarvin.
- **Strength:** Load-bearing. If severed (Yarvin's ideas had no actual influence on policy, DOGE would exist regardless), H1 loses its strongest evidence.

**Bridge 3: Political → Financial**
- **Domains connected:** Political placements → Government contract awards to Thiel portfolio companies
- **Mechanism:** Network members in government (Sacks, O'Neill, Vance's VP role) → favorable procurement environment → $45B+ in Palantir/Anduril contracts
- **Evidence status:** Hypothesized. Temporal correlation is strong (placements precede major contract awards), but no direct evidence of quid pro quo or improper procurement influence. Palantir and Anduril deliver real products. Government contract processes, while imperfect, have formal oversight.
- **Strength:** The leading assessment (H1) survives if this bridge is severed. Ideology can be the primary driver even if financial returns are genuinely earned on merit rather than captured through political influence.

**Bridge 4: Financial → Ideological**
- **Domains connected:** Financial capital → Intellectual infrastructure
- **Mechanism:** Thiel capital (Founders Fund → Tlon, Seasteading) → Yarvin's platform, network-building, salon influence
- **Evidence status:** Confirmed. Founders Fund investment in Tlon is documented. Seasteading funding is documented.
- **Strength:** Central. Without Thiel's patronage, Yarvin would be an eccentric blogger, not a "house philosopher" with access to political power.

**Bridge 5: Technological → Political**
- **Domains connected:** Technology capability → Political leverage
- **Mechanism:** Palantir/Anduril's technology platforms create government dependence (data analytics infrastructure, border surveillance, defense AI), providing ongoing leverage regardless of political cycles
- **Evidence status:** Hypothesized. The scale of contracts ($45B ceiling) and enterprise agreements (consolidating 75+ contracts into single frameworks) suggests technology lock-in, but this is inferred, not directly evidenced through dependency analyses.
- **Strength:** Assessment survives if severed. Government technology dependence is a common phenomenon across vendors, not unique to Thiel-linked companies.

### 6b. Integrated Synthesis (Bridge Hypothesis)

The five bridges converge on a single explanatory thesis:

**Bridge Hypothesis:** Thiel has constructed a self-reinforcing cycle in which ideological capital (Yarvin's anti-democratic framework) provides the intellectual architecture for political placements (Vance, Sacks, O'Neill), political power creates the policy environment (deregulation, procurement reform, DOGE) that benefits Thiel's financial capital (Palantir, Anduril), and financial returns fund the next cycle of ideological and political investment — with ideology as the initiating and sustaining force, not economics.

**Falsifier for the bridge hypothesis:** Evidence that Thiel would sacrifice ideological goals for financial returns — choosing a financially optimal but ideologically neutral path when the two conflict. If Thiel's behavior in a money-vs-ideology tradeoff consistently favors money, the bridge hypothesis breaks because economics, not ideology, is the sustaining force.

**Counter-evidence test:** The Seasteading investment ($1.7M, no return) suggests Thiel does sacrifice financial returns for ideology. However, this is a small-stakes example ($1.7M against a multi-billion-dollar portfolio). The high-stakes test would be whether Thiel would walk away from Palantir's government contracts if they required abandoning his ideological project — and that test has not occurred, because the two have never been in tension. The ideological project and the financial returns currently reinforce each other.

## Assessment

### Leading Assessment

**H1 (Ideology-First Pipeline) leads with 0 inconsistencies, at a distance of 0-to-2 from the nearest competitor (H2).**

The evidence is most consistent with ideology as the primary driver of the Thiel-Yarvin network. The temporal sequence (ideology articulated 2007-2012, political investments 2021-2022, financial returns 2025-2026) supports ideology driving capital, not the reverse. The Seasteading investment demonstrates willingness to spend capital on ideological projects with no financial return. Yarvin's genuine dissatisfaction with DOGE's execution — wanting *more* disruption, not less — contradicts the economics-first model, where DOGE's deregulatory output should satisfy an economically motivated actor.

The distance of 0-to-2 is a moderate call. Two inconsistencies separate H1 from H2, and both rest on evidence items (Seasteading, Yarvin's DOGE criticism) that have plausible reversal scenarios. A skeptical reviewer could argue that both items are ambiguous rather than inconsistent, which would restore the prior report's H1/H2 tie. However, the scoring is conservative — both items represent genuine contradictions of H2's predictions, not mere absence of expected evidence.

### Confidence Levels

**HIGH CONFIDENCE (what we know):**
- The Yarvin-Thiel association is financially documented, ideologically aligned, and politically consequential (Founders Fund → Tlon, $15M → Vance, transition team participation)
- The temporal sequence: anti-democratic ideology (2007-2009) preceded political investments (2021-2022) preceded financial returns (2025-2026)
- Thiel network members occupy operationally significant government positions (VP, AI/Crypto Czar, Deputy HHS Secretary)
- Thiel-linked companies hold $45B+ in government contract ceilings
- The Thiel donor model is structurally different from Koch/Soros/Bloomberg operations

**MODERATE CONFIDENCE (what we assess):**
- Ideology is the primary driver, not economics (0-to-2 inconsistency lead)
- The Financial → Political → Financial circuit is a self-reinforcing cycle, not a one-directional investment
- Yarvin's intellectual framework is actively shaping policy through DOGE and the broader administrative-state dismantlement agenda
- The network operates through directional alignment rather than centralized command-and-control

**LOW CONFIDENCE / GAPS (what we don't know):**
- Whether Thiel and Yarvin actively coordinate on specific political strategies or merely share a worldview that produces convergent action
- The full extent of Thiel network placements below the cabinet/senior level
- Whether Palantir/Anduril contract wins reflect genuine merit-based procurement or are influenced by political positioning
- Whether Thiel would sacrifice financial returns for ideological goals at high stakes (Seasteading is small-stakes evidence)
- The precise mechanism of ideological transmission: do policymakers read Yarvin because Thiel promotes him, or because his ideas are independently compelling to a self-selecting audience?

### Alternative Hypotheses

**H2 (Economics-First with Ideological Cover) — 2 inconsistencies.** H2 is not eliminated but is disconfirmed by two discriminating items. The Seasteading investment ($1.7M with no return pathway) is inconsistent with a rationally self-interested actor for whom ideology is merely cover. Yarvin's genuine dissatisfaction with DOGE (wanting more radical disruption despite DOGE delivering deregulation and contract opportunities) is inconsistent with an economics-first actor who should be satisfied with the financial output. However, H2 remains the nearest competitor — a reviewer who scores both items as Ambiguous rather than Inconsistent restores the tie.

**H3 (Genuine Parallel Pursuit) — 5 inconsistencies.** Five evidence items show deliberate coordination between ideological and financial activities: Thiel introducing Vance to Trump (active pipeline, not parallel tracks), DOGE advisers citing Yarvin (ideological track feeds policy track), Palantir advising on surveillance regulation (financial track and political track converging), "everyone reads Yarvin" (shared intellectual architecture), and Thiel vetting appointments (coordination across all domains). The parallel-tracks model cannot explain this degree of integration.

**H4 (Standard Donor Dynamics) — 7 inconsistencies.** The null case is heavily eliminated. The Thiel network differs from standard mega-donor operations in seven documented ways: an anti-democratic manifesto (normal donors operate within democratic frameworks), a 13-year ideology-to-policy pipeline (RAGE→DOGE), the largest single Senate donation in history, building a VP from scratch, government employees citing the patron's philosopher, a structurally different VC-based donor model, and funding floating libertarian nations. No other mega-donor exhibits this combination.
