## Collection Plan: Aerospace-Nuclear-UAP Anomalous Attrition Investigation

Classification: UNCLASSIFIED — External Analyst Position
Constraint: All collection achievable through OSINT, open-source HUMINT, and public-records analysis
Time sensitivity: HIGH — FBI report expected near-term; collection must precede or parallel federal findings

---

## PIR 1
Question: Is the anomalous attrition rate among cleared aerospace-nuclear personnel statistically significant when measured against the actual actuarial baseline for this workforce?
Decision: Determines whether the cluster warrants investigation as a coordinated phenomenon or falls within expected statistical variation — the foundational question that gates all subsequent analysis
Deadline: Before FBI report release (estimated weeks)
Priority: P1

### SIR 1.1: What is the general-population baseline rate of violent death, accidental overdose death, and unresolved adult disappearance among the demographic cohort that matches cleared scientific personnel (age 35-80, postgraduate education, stable employment, income >$100K)?
EEIs:
- 1.1.1 CDC WONDER database age-adjusted suicide rate for males and females aged 35-80 with doctoral-level education, 2019-2025 — OSINT (public health data) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 1.1.2 Bureau of Justice Statistics homicide victimization rate for the same demographic cohort, 2019-2025 — OSINT (DOJ published data) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 1.1.3 National Missing and Unidentified Persons System (NamUs) resolution rate for adults aged 50+ reported missing in New Mexico and California, 2020-2026 — OSINT (public database) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 1.1.4 Published actuarial studies or workforce-attrition reports from DOE, NNSA, or national-laboratory employee unions that report mortality rates among laboratory employees — OSINT (FOIA-released documents, GAO reports, union publications) — LTOV: 3 weeks

### SIR 1.2: What is the size of the population at risk — the number of individuals with current or recent Q/L clearances or TS/SCI access at aerospace and nuclear-weapons-complex facilities?
EEIs:
- 1.2.1 ODNI Annual Statistical Transparency Report figures for total active TS/SCI clearances, 2020-2025 — OSINT (ODNI published reports) — LTOV: 1 week
- 1.2.2 DOE/NNSA workforce data for national laboratories (LANL, Sandia, LLNL, ORNL, INL) total cleared employees, published in annual site reports or congressional budget justifications — OSINT (congressional submissions) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 1.2.3 NASA/JPL total workforce with facility security clearances, published in annual workforce reports or FOIA-released organizational charts — OSINT — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 1.2.4 Disconfirming: Published analysis by any credentialed statistician or epidemiologist arguing the cluster falls within baseline expectations — OSINT (academic preprints, media commentary, fact-check articles) — LTOV: 1 week

### SIR 1.3: How does the observed rate of 11+ events across 4 years compare to the expected rate for this population?
EEIs:
- 1.3.1 Independent calculation: given the population denominator from SIR 1.2 and the baseline rates from SIR 1.1, what is the expected number of violent deaths, overdose deaths, and unresolved disappearances per 4-year period? — ANALYSIS (derived from collected EEIs) — LTOV: 1 week after SIR 1.1/1.2 collection complete
- 1.3.2 Monte Carlo or Poisson-distribution simulation of geographic and temporal clustering probability given the facility locations and the baseline rate — ANALYSIS — LTOV: 2 weeks after SIR 1.1/1.2

## PIR 2
Question: Do any of the 11+ cases under federal investigation show foreign-intelligence indicators that would support the foreign-actor hypothesis?
Decision: Determines whether the investigation should orient toward counterespionage or toward domestic/structural explanations
Deadline: Before FBI report release
Priority: P1

### SIR 2.1: Do any of the affected individuals have documented foreign contacts, travel to adversary nations, or participation in international academic exchange programs that are known vectors for foreign-intelligence recruitment?
EEIs:
- 2.1.1 Published academic collaboration records (co-authored papers, joint conference presentations, visiting-researcher appointments) for each of the 11+ individuals, cross-referenced against institutions in countries with documented intelligence-collection programs targeting US aerospace/nuclear personnel — OSINT (academic databases: Google Scholar, ORCID, institutional publication lists) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 2.1.2 LinkedIn, ResearchGate, and professional-network profiles of each individual showing international affiliations, advisory roles, or speaking engagements in adversary-nation institutions — OSINT — LTOV: 1 week
- 2.1.3 FOIA requests to DOE and DoD for any counterintelligence incident reports or security-concern reports filed regarding any of the affected individuals (will likely be denied or heavily redacted, but the denial itself is informative) — OSINT (FOIA) — LTOV: 6-12 months (low priority given timeline)
- 2.1.4 Disconfirming: Published statements by FBI or DOE CI officials explicitly ruling out foreign-intelligence involvement in any of the cases — OSINT — LTOV: ongoing

### SIR 2.2: Does the geographic concentration of cases correlate with known foreign-intelligence collection priorities and operational presence?
EEIs:
- 2.2.1 Published DOJ/FBI espionage prosecution records for the Los Alamos/Albuquerque area, 2015-2026, as a measure of adversary operational presence — OSINT (DOJ press releases, court records via PACER) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 2.2.2 Published DOJ/FBI espionage prosecution records for the greater Los Angeles area including Pasadena/JPL corridor, 2015-2026 — OSINT — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 2.2.3 CSIS espionage-case database entries involving aerospace or nuclear targets in NM or CA — OSINT (published database) — LTOV: 1 week
- 2.2.4 Disconfirming: If espionage prosecution density in these areas is proportionate to their share of the national cleared workforce, geographic clustering is expected and not indicative of targeted foreign operations — ANALYSIS — LTOV: 2 weeks

## PIR 3
Question: Is there a causal relationship between individuals' proximity to legislative/legal testimony on classified programs and their subsequent death or disappearance?
Decision: Determines whether a protective-witness framework is needed for future congressional oversight activities
Deadline: Immediate — upcoming hearings may place additional individuals at risk
Priority: P1

### SIR 3.1: How many individuals have been scheduled, invited, or have volunteered to testify before Congress or in legal proceedings regarding classified aerospace/UAP programs since 2020, and what is the mortality/disappearance rate among that group versus the non-testifying cleared population?
EEIs:
- 3.1.1 Complete list of individuals who have testified, been scheduled to testify, or publicly offered to testify before congressional committees on UAP/classified aerospace programs, 2020-2026 — OSINT (congressional hearing records, C-SPAN archives, media reporting, advocacy-community records) — LTOV: 1 week
- 3.1.2 Current status (alive/deceased/missing) and circumstances for each individual on the list — OSINT (public records, media, social media activity) — LTOV: 1 week
- 3.1.3 For the deceased/missing subset: precise dates of death/disappearance relative to scheduled testimony dates, measured in days — OSINT (court dockets, hearing schedules, death records) — LTOV: 1 week
- 3.1.4 Disconfirming: Individuals who testified and experienced no adverse events afterward — the survivor set is as important as the casualty set for calculating the true rate — OSINT — LTOV: 1 week

### SIR 3.2: Is the pre-testimony death pattern statistically distinguishable from the background rate of death among individuals under high professional stress (whistleblowers, litigants, witnesses in high-profile proceedings)?
EEIs:
- 3.2.1 Published studies on mortality, suicide, and health outcomes among whistleblowers in any sector (healthcare, financial, defense), as a comparison population — OSINT (academic databases, PubMed, SSRN) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 3.2.2 Published data on witness deaths before testimony in federal cases generally (DOJ Inspector General reports, academic legal scholarship) — OSINT — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 3.2.3 Disconfirming: If the general whistleblower/witness population shows a similarly elevated pre-testimony mortality rate, the pattern may reflect stress rather than targeting — ANALYSIS — LTOV: 3 weeks

## PIR 4
Question: Do the "essentials-absent" disappearances reflect voluntary crisis behavior, coerced removal, or a combination?
Decision: Determines whether the disappearance cases require criminal investigation (coercion) or welfare-check protocols (voluntary departure under duress)
Deadline: Ongoing — new cases may occur
Priority: P2

### SIR 4.1: What is the behavioral baseline for adults who voluntarily leave their homes during a mental health crisis — do they typically take personal effects, and what is the recovery/resolution rate?
EEIs:
- 4.1.1 Published missing-persons behavioral studies (FBI's NCIC data, NamUs case analyses, academic forensic-psychology literature) on the personal-effects profile of adults who leave voluntarily versus those who are removed by force — OSINT (academic databases, law-enforcement publications) — LTOV: 2 weeks
- 4.1.2 Specific case-comparison data: in resolved missing-persons cases involving adults aged 40-80 who left without personal effects, what percentage were found alive, found deceased (exposure, accident, suicide), or never found? — OSINT (NamUs, published case studies) — LTOV: 3 weeks

### SIR 4.2: Do the specific circumstances of the essentials-absent disappearances match the voluntary-crisis profile or deviate from it?
EEIs:
- 4.2.1 For each disappearance case: was the individual's vehicle at home? Were house doors locked or unlocked? Were any items packed or removed? Was there any digital activity (calls, texts, card transactions) after the last sighting? — OSINT (police reports released to media, family statements, press reporting) — LTOV: 1 week
- 4.2.2 For the McCasland case specifically: were prescription glasses left behind (suggesting departure was not self-initiated, as a person dependent on corrective lenses would not voluntarily leave without them)? What was his last known location via any digital trace? — OSINT (press reporting, family statements) — LTOV: 1 week
- 4.2.3 Disconfirming: Evidence that any of the missing individuals had previously discussed wanting to disappear, had financial preparations for departure, or had a history of similar episodes — OSINT/HUMINT (family statements, press interviews) — LTOV: 2 weeks

## Priority Gaps

1. **Actuarial baseline** — Load-bearing: without it, the statistical-significance question (PIR-1) cannot be definitively answered. Recommended action: construct the best available proxy from demographic-matched public-health data and clearly caveat the structural limitation. This is the single most important gap for analytic credibility.

2. **Complete witness roster and status** — Load-bearing: the pre-testimony pattern (PIR-3) cannot be validated without knowing the full population of individuals who have been scheduled or willing to testify, including those who testified without incident. Recommended action: systematic OSINT compilation from congressional records, media, and advocacy sources. Achievable within 1 week.

3. **Digital forensics for affected individuals** — Load-bearing: the distinction between voluntary departure and coerced removal (PIR-4) ultimately requires device and communications analysis. Recommended action: flag as a gap that only the federal investigation can close; ensure it is prominently noted in the I&W matrix as a collection requirement for any future case.

4. **Current threat reporting from potential witnesses** — Load-bearing: the protective-posture question (PIR-5) requires real-time awareness of threats against identifiable at-risk individuals. Recommended action: establish continuous OSINT monitoring of named potential witnesses' public communications and congressional correspondence.
